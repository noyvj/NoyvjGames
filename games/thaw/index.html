Manage a northern region as global temperature rises on its own. Watch for the moment a slow problem tips into a runaway feedback loop.

Permafrost holds an enormous store of carbon and methane built up over thousands of years. As it thaws, that stored gas releases and adds to warming — a real feedback loop, and one of the clearest examples of climate change accelerating itself once a threshold is crossed.

    The fixed background rise stands in for a much longer real-world timescale, compressed for pacing — real global average temperature has risen roughly 1.1°C over the last century and a half, not in single fixed jumps per round. At today's warming pace (NOAA: about 0.20°C per decade since 1982, i.e. 0.02°C/year), this game's fixed +1.0°/round works out to roughly 50 years of real-world warming compressed into one round. Treat each round as a stylized several decades of real warming, not a literal calendar unit.

    Permafrost stable — no feedback yet.

    i

    Permafrost starts melting once global temperature crosses +10.0° — below that threshold it's dormant, but above it every extra degree releases methane that adds to next round's warming, on top of the fixed background rise. Past a much faster acceleration, melt status reads as "critical" instead of just "melting" — the feedback loop compounding fast rather than just having started.

    i

    A tipping event is permafrost starting to melt, or warming going critical (2x the background rate). This counts rounds since the last one. The +15° milestone is the second warming marker: past it, the feedback loop is releasing more methane than intervention alone can easily offset.

    i

    Dampening only reduces the methane feedback bonus caused by melt — it never touches the fixed background warming rate, which always rises no matter what you invest in. That's also why this percentage can sit above 0% with no visible effect yet: it does nothing until melt actually starts.

Loop

Redesign a supply chain. By default it's a straight line: extract, make, use, discard. Waste is a design choice — not an inevitability.

Overconsumption and the take-make-waste model carry a real extraction and emissions cost — the raw materials behind everyday electronics (metals, minerals, rare earths) are finite and increasingly costly to keep extracting at current rates.

    Environmental damage

    i

    Every unit of new extraction leaves lasting damage that never resets — once cumulative extraction is high enough, further extraction costs up to 2.5x more per unit. There's no hard fail-state, but the only way to avoid feeding this meter at all is circularity investment, since it substitutes for new extraction instead of just paying its cost.

    i

    The three investments aren't interchangeable — each supplies a different amount toward the production target per unit bought: Repair Networks give 3 units (cost 20), Reuse Systems give 4 units (cost 25), and Recycling Loops give 5 units (cost 30), roughly reflecting how close each stage sits to recovering material that would otherwise be pure waste.

    Trade network

    i

    The Trade Link works both ways: it imports reuse capacity from a neighboring system that counts toward closing your loop, and separately, whenever your own circularity investments produce more supply than this cycle's production target needs, that surplus is automatically sold outward for extra revenue instead of going to waste.

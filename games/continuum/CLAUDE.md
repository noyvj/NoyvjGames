# Continuum — A Sustainable City Across the Ages

**Working title — kept as-is (no better name landed, see naming note at the bottom).** This is the single flagship Digital Artefact for BCM114 Round 2, built around SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities). Unlike Round 1's speculative-media framing (Theme 9), **this round's framing is fictional media with a real story running through it** — not a warning about a possible future, a narrative about a specific city and its people across time. Unlike the Round 1 climate quartet (many small games, teaching-forward), **this is one deep game, and it should read as a game first** — the SDG 11 elements should live in what makes a good city good to live in, not in explicit lessons. Read this whole file before starting any milestone.

**Explicitly standalone:** no narrative or systemic connection to SOL or any other hub game. Shares only the hub's general conventions (testing, tech stack, hosting), not lore or world-state. (A generational "descendants" narrative thread was considered and deferred — see Deferred Items.)

**Design philosophy this round (important shift from the climate quartet):** less overt teaching, more game. No end-of-session survey prompts, no separate "did this feel effective" questions bolted onto the UI. Real-world grounding lives behind a collapsed, optional info panel — never on the main screen. The sustainability/livability balance should be something the player discovers through play, the way a good city-builder makes you feel the cost of bad zoning without a tooltip explaining it.

## Concept

The player grows a single settlement from a small tribal community into a space-age civilization, across seven eras, developing infrastructure, technology, and culture along the way — all while a visible sustainability/livability score tracks whether that growth is actually good to live inside, not just big. A real story follows the city and (loosely) its people across the eras, told through the log and transition-beat systems below.

## Eras (confirmed)

1. **Tribal** — small settlement, subsistence, earliest social organization
2. **Agrarian** — settled agriculture, early surplus and trade
3. **Classical** — early urbanization, civic institutions, early infrastructure
4. **Medieval** — denser urban centers, guilds/specialization, early public works
5. **Industrial** — mechanization, rapid growth, the era where sustainability tension becomes most visible historically
6. **Current/Digital** — modern city systems, information infrastructure, present-day sustainability challenges
7. **Space Age** — future era, off-world or post-scarcity infrastructure questions

**Build order (dev milestones) runs Tribal → Space Age**, matching play order — simplest era first, foundational systems proven before later eras build on top of them.

## Core systems (apply across all eras)

### 1. City simulation core
Population, resources, infrastructure, and production, scaled appropriately per era (a tribal settlement's "resources" are food/shelter/tools; a space-age city's are wildly different) but built on the same underlying simulation engine so eras share code rather than being seven separate games stitched together.

### 2. Research tree (one continuous tree spanning all eras)
A single tree that grows across the whole game — later eras unlock deeper branches rather than replacing the tree each era. Early-era research choices should have visible echoes in what's available later. Design it in tiers/layers from the start (Milestone 3) before populating individual nodes, so later eras don't require retrofitting the whole tree's shape.

### 3. Sustainability/Livability score — THE core teaching mechanism, not a side meter
This is the single most important design principle for the whole game: **the growth-vs-sustainability tension has to be what actually teaches, by being built into the win condition itself — not displayed to the player as a lesson.** This mirrors how Herd's coupling ratio worked in the climate quartet: the score has to directly gate real things the player wants (how large a city can grow before instability caps it, whether population migrates in or out, whether unrest or decay events start firing) rather than sitting next to the "real" score as a decorative sustainability rating. If a player can ignore livability and still win by raw growth alone, this system has failed its one job.

Concretely: an unsustainable city should hit a real ceiling — migration outflow, unrest events, infrastructure decay compounding — not a scolding message. A well-balanced city should be able to grow larger and more stable than an unsustainable one of the same era, provably, the same way Herd's decoupled farm outscored the pure-growth farm 412 to 52.

### 4. Story system (mix: light ongoing logs + bigger era-transition beats)
- **Ongoing logs:** short flavor text tied to milestones within an era (a research unlock, a population threshold, a livability shift) — lightweight, non-blocking, skippable.
- **Era-transition beats:** a bigger narrative moment when the city crosses into a new era.
- **This is also where feedback lives now** (replacing the climate quartet's separate end-of-session survey prompts): player reactions to livability shifts should surface through the story itself — a log entry reacting to a housing shortage, a transition beat referencing how the city handled its last era's growing pains — rather than a standalone "did this feel effective?" question. Feedback is diegetic, woven into what the city's story is already telling the player, not a separate system bolted on top.
- Keep the fictional framing generic enough to be edited for tone later — build the system first, refine prose during a dedicated pass.

### 5. Save system (continuous save, revisit completed eras)
One continuous save spanning the whole arc, with the ability to revisit/replay completed eras without losing forward progress. Use the existing Neon/FastAPI backend for this rather than browser storage. Plan the save schema early (Milestone 2 or 3).

**Given how failure-prone save-merge logic was across the entire climate quartet** (a systemic wholesale-dict-replace bug that hid in "clean" audits until a second, harder pass caught it in every single game), Continuum's save-revisit system needs its own dedicated security/bug audit pass once built — not just folded into general testing. See Milestone list below.

### 6. Real-world info panel (collapsed by default)
A single "Info" button, always available but never intrusive — tapping it expands a panel with real-world sources for the current era (see the companion research doc, `continuum-real-world-sources.md`). Collapsed by default specifically to avoid overloading the screen or forcing scroll on mobile — the main screen should show gameplay only. This replaces the climate quartet's per-game "Learn More" page pattern with something more compact, since Continuum is one continuous game rather than eight separate short ones.

**Not yet built** — no info panel exists in the shipped Phase 1 code. `continuum-real-world-sources.md` is landed as reference content only; wiring it up (era-aware, collapsed-by-default) is Phase 2/3 scope, built alongside each era's own content per the Phase 3 pattern below.

### 7. Visual style: low-poly, cartoonish 3D
Achievable, but it's an architecture decision worth being explicit about: **Pyodide alone can't render 3D** — this requires a hybrid setup, with game state and logic staying in Python (testable, consistent with the rest of the hub) while a Three.js layer handles rendering. Low-poly/cartoonish is a good style match for this constraint — flat-shaded geometry, simple toon-style shading, and simplified geometric forms per era (a cluster of huts for Tribal, blocky spires for Space Age) are all very achievable in Three.js without needing realistic textures or complex asset pipelines. This is a genuine scope increase over the climate quartet's plain HTML/Pyodide games — budget for it as its own milestone track (Phase 5), not a drop-in visual pass at the end. Supersedes the space-theme glass-panel visual pass Phase 1 shipped with (see the build note below) — that pass was a stopgap consistent with the rest of the hub, not the intended final visual direction.

### 8. Accessibility
Colorblind-safe palette and text scaling, built in from the visual pass rather than retrofitted. Worth doing well here specifically since it's being flagged as a pattern to eventually apply site-wide (see Deferred Items) — Continuum can be the first implementation to get right.

## Phase 1 build decisions

Concrete calls made while building Phase 1 that the doc left open. Revisit freely — these are decisions, not constraints.

- **File layout: separate engine modules, still no build step.** The tech notes ask for the simulation core, research tree and save system to be "clearly separated modules from the start," so Continuum is split into `sim.py` / `sustainability.py` / `research.py` / `save.py` with `game.py` as a thin browser layer that owns the DOM and nothing else. Every other hub game is a single `game.py`; this is the documented exception. `index.html` fetches each engine module and writes it into Pyodide's virtual filesystem before running `game.py`, which imports them normally — more fetches, still no bundler.
- **The simulation is fully deterministic.** No RNG anywhere in the season loop. Randomised events are a Phase 2/3 question; determinism is what keeps era snapshots and revisits testable.
- **Research reaches the simulation through exactly one seam.** `sim.NEUTRAL_EFFECTS` defines every modifier key the tree is allowed to touch, and `advance_season(effects)` applies them. Milestone 1 always passes the neutral dict, so the tree could be added in Milestone 3 without editing the season loop.
- **Era list confirmed as proposed** (Tribal → Agrarian → Classical → Medieval → Industrial → Digital → Space Age), and lives in `sim.ERA_ORDER`. The research tree's tier map and the save file's era-snapshot dict both key off that one list, so changing an era later means editing one constant.
- **Tribal-era shape.** Four worker roles (foragers / gatherers / crafters / keepers) and four buildings (shelter / storage pit / fire circle / knapping site). Keepers produce `knowledge`, which is the research currency for all seven eras — one currency across the whole tree rather than an era-specific one.
- **The score is built from ratios only, never counts.** Every input to `sustainability.py` is a per-capita or per-limit ratio, which is what mechanically guarantees the doc's requirement that a small well-run settlement can beat a large badly-run one. Scaling a settlement up while scaling what it needs up with it is score-neutral by construction; there is a test that asserts exactly that.
- **Four equally-weighted components** — livability, equity, resource balance, resilience — matching the doc's SDG 11 framing. Equal weighting is a judgement call, not a finding; `sustainability.COMPONENT_WEIGHTS` is the one place to revisit it.
- **Equity is bounded by the least-met need, not the average.** A settlement with grand shelters and empty stomachs scores badly on equity even where its average provision looks fine. This is what keeps equity from being a restatement of livability.
- **The score is a pure function of state.** Nothing in `sustainability.py` mutates anything or accumulates hidden history, so any era snapshot can be re-scored at any time — which is what makes Milestone 4's revisit feature cheap. The score *history* is state and lives on `CityState`.
- **Research tier structure: two tiers per era, 14 global tiers.** This is the tier proposal the doc asks for at Milestone 3. Tier numbers are global and ascending (`research.era_tiers(era)` / `research.tier_era(tier)`), so a node never gets renumbered when a later era is written. A tier opens once `TIER_UNLOCK_REQUIREMENT` (currently 2) nodes in the tier below are researched — a soft gate, so a player can specialise and still progress. Era gating sits on top: nodes from an era the settlement hasn't reached are never available.
- **Three branches spanning all seven eras** — provision / community / craft — rather than per-era categories, which is what makes "early choices echo later" mechanically real. Two mechanisms carry an emphasis forward: ordinary `prerequisites`, and `min_affinity`, which gates a node on how many nodes in a branch have been researched at all. `elders_council` is the shipped proof of the second one.
- **Node effects are always deltas.** `{"food_yield_mult": 0.2}` means +20%; the tree sums deltas onto `sim.NEUTRAL_EFFECTS` (multiplicative keys based at 1.0, additive at 0.0). Uniform aggregation, no per-key special cases as the tree grows to 14 tiers.
- **Knowledge is one currency for the whole tree**, produced by the Keeper role in the Tribal era and by whatever the equivalent is later. Research is never bought with era-specific resources.
- **The shipped tree is asserted structurally valid in tests.** `ResearchTree.validate()` catches unknown prerequisites, prerequisites from later tiers, prerequisite cycles, unknown effect keys, and tiers that don't belong to their era. Content bugs in a 14-tier tree are cheap to make and expensive to find by playing.
- **The research panel is rendered in code, not written into `index.html`.** Locked nodes are listed too, with the reason they're locked, so the tree reads as a tree rather than a queue.
- **Land health is the sustainability engine, not a decoration.** The land has a finite sustainable yield per season; harvesting past it degrades land health, which cuts every future yield, and staying under it lets the land recover. This is the mechanism that makes a small well-run settlement able to out-score a large badly-run one.
- **Saving goes through the shared hub widget, not a bespoke bridge.** This supersedes the doc's Milestone 4 wording above: the backend is still the existing FastAPI/Neon save system, but it's reached via `shared/save-widget.js` (see `planning/SAVE-BUTTON-INTEGRATION.md`), which every hub game includes unchanged. Continuum's whole per-game obligation is `get_state()` / `load_state(data)` in `game.py`; the widget never looks inside the dict, so the entire era-snapshot/revisit structure lives *inside* that one dict and needs no widget changes.
- **Save schema.** `{save_version, game, era_order, current_era, furthest_era, revisiting, current_state: {city, research}, parked_state, era_snapshots: {era: {...}}, ui}`. `current_state` is always whatever is being played; entering a revisit parks live forward progress in `parked_state` and loads the requested era's snapshot; leaving puts the parked state back. `furthest_era` never moves backwards.
- **A revisit is a look back, not a rewrite.** Leaving a revisit discards what happened during it, and a completed era's snapshot is never overwritten by a replay — the record of how an era actually went the first time is what the game's "what kind of city did you build" framing rests on. Worth reconsidering in Phase 2 if replaying an era should be able to *change* the arc.
- **The save system restores in place.** `Campaign` mutates the `CityState` and `ResearchTree` it holds rather than replacing them, so `game.py`'s module-level `state`/`tree` aliases survive a load.
- **`Campaign.advance_to_era()` exists but nothing calls it yet.** Phase 2's era-transition beat system is what will drive it; only the save-side bookkeeping (snapshot the era being left, move `furthest_era`, retarget the research tree) is built and tested now, since that's the part that would have been painful to retrofit.
- **`CITY_FIELDS` in `save.py` is an explicit list, not a `__dict__` sweep.** Adding a field to `CityState` is therefore a deliberate save-schema decision. If a future field doesn't persist, that list is the first place to look.
- **A save carries values, not shape (audit fix).** `CITY_KEYED_DICTS` in `save.py` names the three fields — `resources`, `allocation`, `buildings` — whose *key set* belongs to `sim.py` rather than to the save file. They are now restored key by key into the live dicts instead of replacing them. Before this, a save written today and loaded by a build that had since added a role, a building or a resource left the settlement with no entry for it, and the next season or render died on a `KeyError` — *after* the save widget had already reported a successful load, which is the worst version of that failure. The same pass gave the live half of the save the type-guarding `parked_state`/`era_snapshots`/`ui` already had, made `ResearchTree.restore()` tolerate a non-list, and made `game.py`'s `season_report_message()` read `last_report` with `.get()`, since a saved report can predate whatever keys the current season loop writes. This is the "old save, newer build" half of the forward compatibility the schema was designed early for; the "newer save, older build" half was already handled by the `save_version` refusal.
- **A second audit pass (three follow-up fixes left out of scope the first time).**
  - `render_research()` minted a fresh `create_proxy()`-wrapped click handler for every unresearched node's Study button on *every* render, and never destroyed the one it was replacing — a real Pyodide proxy isn't garbage-collected on its own, so this was a slow memory leak over a play session. `game.py` now tracks the live proxy per `node_id` in a module-level dict and `.destroy()`s the previous one before installing its replacement (and destroys it outright once a node no longer needs a button at all, i.e. once it's researched). Verified by a test against the fake-DOM harness — `tests/fakes.py`'s `create_proxy()` fake now attaches a real `.destroy()`/`.destroyed` onto the wrapped function so a test can assert this without a real Pyodide runtime.
  - `sim.ROLE_LABEL`/`ROLE_BLURB`/`BUILDING_LABEL`/`BUILDING_BLURB` were dead tables — `index.html` hard-coded the identical strings as static markup instead of reading them, left that way deliberately until "the moment a second era ships." Wiring them up turned out to be small and safe (index.html already showed the identical text, so the two per-role/per-building loops already in `render()` just needed two more lines each, plus new `id`s on the name/blurb elements — no visible change, verified against the pytest suite and a rendered-page text diff). Done now rather than left staged: `game.py`'s `render()` is the one place this text comes from, matching how the research panel already reads `research.BRANCH_LABEL`.
  - `Campaign.load_dict()` restored `revisiting` by checking only that it named a valid era, not that `era_snapshots` actually had a snapshot for it — non-exploitable today because `enter_revisit()` is the only normal path that sets `revisiting`, and it never does so without a matching snapshot, and because the existing `parked_state`-is-missing guard already catches a save that drops both fields together. But that guard doesn't fire when a hand-edited save keeps a `parked_state` while pointing `revisiting` at an era `era_snapshots` has no entry for. Added a defensive cross-check (`revisiting not in self.era_snapshots` clears both fields) — belt-and-suspenders, asserted by a test but shouldn't change behaviour for any currently-reachable state.
  - Also removed `sustainability.COMPONENT_BLURB`: unlike the role/building tables above, it had no static-markup counterpart anywhere and no comment explaining why it was kept — genuinely dead, so it went rather than staying unexplained.

## Milestone 5 build decisions (collapsed real-world info panel)

- **Reused `shared/info_page.py` unchanged, rather than writing a bespoke panel.** The doc explicitly leaves this open ("you do NOT have to use that exact shared module if Continuum's own per-era structure doesn't fit it cleanly"), but it turned out to fit cleanly once inverted: the shared module's `render(info_page_data, is_open)` already takes a plain `{"framing", "mechanic_tie_in", "sources"}` dict and doesn't care where it came from. The climate quartet has one static dict per game; Continuum instead has a new `info_content.py` module with one such dict *per era* (`ERA_INFO_PAGE`), and a single lookup function, `era_info_page(era)`, that game.py calls every render. Reusing the shared module means the panel's DOM structure, CSS (`shared/info-page.css`), and toggle behaviour are identical to the 8 climate-quartet games with zero duplicated rendering code — the only Continuum-specific code is the era lookup.
- **`info_content.py` is a new dedicated module, not folded into `game.py`.** It's pure data (no DOM calls), so it follows the same "engine modules stay separate, game.py stays a thin browser layer" rule Phase 1 already established for `sim.py`/`sustainability.py`/`research.py`/`save.py` — it just happens to be data rather than logic. It doesn't belong in any of the four existing engine modules either: it has nothing to do with simulation, scoring, research or save schema.
- **Content is keyed by era, and only `tribal` has a real entry — matching `sim.IMPLEMENTED_ERAS`.** The doc's own instruction was to use `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s real Tribal content rather than a placeholder, while staying era-aware for the six eras Phase 3 hasn't built yet. `era_info_page()` falls back to a small `_PENDING` dict (a plain "hasn't been written yet" framing line, empty sources list) for any era without an entry, so loading a save against a build that has since reached a later era degrades gracefully instead of raising `KeyError` — the same forward-compatibility standard Milestone 4's audit held the save system to. Adding a second era's content later is exactly one new entry in `ERA_INFO_PAGE`; nothing else changes.
- **The panel is genuinely era-aware, not just era-aware-at-open-time.** `render_info_page()` is called from inside `render()` (same as `render_sustainability()`/`render_research()`), and always re-reads `state.era` — so if the settlement crosses an era boundary (Milestone 7/8) while the panel happens to be open, the content updates in place on the very next render rather than requiring the player to close and reopen it. Verified live: forcing `state.era` to `"agrarian"` mid-session and re-rendering swaps the panel to the pending-placeholder text without touching the toggle state.
- **The open/closed flag is persisted through `campaign.ui`, not a new save field.** The schema already has a `ui` dict explicitly documented as "view toggles worth preserving" (see the Phase 1 Save schema note above) — `info_page_open` is exactly that, so it rides there (`campaign.ui["info_page_open"]` set in `get_state()`, read back with `.get(..., False)` in `load_state()`) rather than needing a schema change. An old save with no such key loads as closed, not an error — tested directly.
- **Placement matches the climate quartet's own convention**: the toggle button sits right after the tagline/context blurb, before the first gameplay section, with the collapsed panel directly under it — same position as every climate-quartet game's "The Real Story" button, so a player who already knows that pattern from another game on the hub finds it in the same place here.
- **Verified live under Pyodide** (not just the fake-DOM suite): the button reads real text (not stuck on "Loading...") once Pyodide boots, confirming `import info_page` and `import info_content` both resolved from the virtual filesystem; toggling shows the real Tribal framing/tie-in text and all four sources as working links (`target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, correct hrefs matching `continuum-real-world-sources.md`); the panel survives a season advance while open; forcing the era to `"agrarian"` and re-rendering swaps in the pending placeholder. Only console output was the pre-existing service-worker-registration error common to every hub game in the preview sandbox. Test count: 99 → 107 (8 new tests in `tests/test_info_page.py`).

## Milestone 6 build decisions (ongoing log system)

- **A new `log.py` engine module, owned by `save.Campaign` as `campaign.log`.** Same separation-of-concerns rule as everything else in Phase 1: the log's "have we already reported this" bookkeeping is state, so it can't live in `game.py` (which is DOM-only), and it isn't `CityState`/`ResearchTree`'s concern either — those stay free of anything to do with narration. `save.py` composes a `log.Chronicle()` into `Campaign.__init__` alongside `state`/`tree`, the same way it already composes those two, and `Chronicle` is mutated in place across a load (never replaced) so `game.py`'s `chronicle = campaign.log` alias survives a load exactly like the existing `state`/`tree` aliases do.
- **Three trigger conditions, matching the doc's milestone description literally**: a research unlock, a population threshold, and a livability shift. Research fires immediately when `tree.research()` is called (from `_make_research_handler`), not deferred to the next season, since research can happen mid-season and the doc's framing ("a research unlock") reads as an event, not a season-end summary. Population and livability are checked once per `on_advance_season()`, since both are properties of a completed season.
- **Population thresholds are a fixed list (`POPULATION_MILESTONES = [10, 15, 25, 40, 60, 100]`), loosely modelled on Dunbar's layered group-size thresholds** — the same real-world basis the Tribal info panel (Milestone 5) already cites — rather than a formula. A fixed list is easy to extend or retune later without touching the checking logic, and reads as deliberate design rather than an arbitrary modulus. Crossing several thresholds in one season (a growth spurt, or a save loaded straight into a much larger population) logs every threshold that was crossed, not just the highest — tested directly, since silently skipping intermediate milestones would be a believable and easy-to-miss bug.
- **Livability shifts are keyed off `sustainability.score_label()` changing, not the raw score.** The score moves every season by small amounts that would make a log entry on every single season advance if the trigger were "the score changed at all" — the five-band label (Collapsing/Failing/Strained/Steady/Thriving) is the same threshold the UI already narrates with (`sustainability.score_label`), so a log entry means "the settlement's *situation* changed," matching the doc's "a livability shift" framing rather than firing on every noise-level fluctuation.
- **This is the diegetic-feedback seam the design doc asks for.** An upward shift gets one of four generic "things got better" lines keyed by the new label; a downward shift gets a line keyed by `sustainability.weakest_component()` — the same function the sustainability panel already uses to explain "what's dragging the score down" — so the log names roughly *what* got worse (people going hungry, an unequal settlement, land overuse, no reserves) rather than a generic "your score decreased." No end-of-session survey, no "did this feel effective" question anywhere.
- **Label text is keyed by `score_label()`'s exact capitalised strings ("Thriving", not "thriving"), on purpose.** This was a real bug caught in testing, not a hypothetical one: an early version of `_LABEL_ORDER` used lower-cased keys while `Chronicle.bootstrap()`/`check_livability()` store whatever `score_label()` actually returns (capitalised), which meant a freshly-saved label failed the `restore()` validity check and silently reset to `None` on every load — three save-system tests caught it immediately (`fresh.to_dict() == data` round-trip assertions). Fixed by keeping `_LABEL_ORDER` and `LIVABILITY_UP_TEXT`'s keys in the exact case `score_label()` produces, documented in `log.py` itself so the next key added there doesn't reintroduce the mismatch.
- **A session-length cap (`log.MAX_ENTRIES = 60`), oldest dropped first**, so a very long play session doesn't grow the save file without bound — a design cap, not a limit on how much can happen; nothing about the trigger logic depends on it. The render path caps independently and more tightly (`game.LOG_VISIBLE_ENTRIES = 20`) so a long game doesn't grow the DOM either; `Chronicle` still holds up to 60 for the save file even when only 20 are ever drawn.
- **The log is one continuous record for the whole campaign — it is not swapped, parked, or reset by `enter_revisit()`/`exit_revisit()`.** Per-era snapshots capture how the city and research tree looked at the time; the log is the settlement's diary of what actually happened, which doesn't change depending on which era you're currently looking back at. This also means the log's `"log"` field sits at the same top level as `"ui"` in the save schema (see `save.py`'s updated schema docstring) rather than nested inside `era_snapshots` or `current_state`.
- **Old-save tolerance, same standard as the Milestone 4 audit.** A save written before this system existed has no `"log"` key at all; `Campaign.load_dict()` re-`bootstrap()`s the Chronicle against whatever state was just restored rather than leaving it empty-and-about-to-replay, so loading an old save doesn't dump the settlement's entire history into the log the moment the next research/season check runs. Tested directly (`test_loading_a_pre_milestone_6_save_bootstraps_instead_of_replaying_history`).
- **Verified live under Pyodide**, including the actual `get_state()`/`load_state()` widget contract (not just `Campaign.to_dict()`/`load_dict()` directly): researching Fire-Keeping, crossing population 10, and forcing a livability downturn each produced the correct log entry live, in the correct newest-first render order; round-tripping through `get_state()` → `json.dumps`/`json.loads` → `load_state()` restored all three entries with no duplicates, and re-running all three checks afterward logged nothing new (confirming the bookkeeping survives a real save/load, not just the in-memory object). One real bug surfaced only by hitting a stale browser cache mid-session (a prior `save.py` fetch had been cached before the Milestone 6 edits landed, so `Campaign` briefly had no `.log` attribute in one browser tab) — resolved by reloading against a fresh port/tab, not a code fix; worth remembering that Pyodide's `fetch()`-based module loading is exactly as cacheable as any other static asset. Test count: 107 → 123 (16 new tests in `tests/test_log_system.py`).

## Milestone 7 build decisions (era-transition beat system)

- **Deliberately not wired into the UI yet — this is the framework only, per its own scope note in `transition.py`.** `sim.py`'s roles/buildings/production are still Tribal-only module-level tables; exposing a working "Advance to Agrarian" control today would let a player flip `state.era` to `"agrarian"` while every role/building label and every production number stayed exactly Tribal — the header would say Agrarian over content that's still mechanically Tribal. That's exactly the "looks shipped, isn't" gap the hub's cadence rule exists to prevent. Milestone 8's own description — "exercising the full save/revisit and transition-beat systems end to end" — is what wires this framework into the UI, once Agrarian has real content to transition *to*. `attempt_transition()` is fully built and fully tested now; it just isn't reachable by clicking anything in the shipped page until Milestone 8.
- **A new `transition.py` engine module, not folded into `save.py` or `log.py`.** It genuinely straddles both — it decides *when* to call `Campaign.advance_to_era()` (a `save.py` concern) and *what* to log when it does (a `log.py` concern) — so giving it its own module keeps `save.py` focused on the save schema's own mechanics and `log.py` focused on its own three season-level triggers, rather than either one growing a second responsibility.
- **Readiness is table-driven per era (`TRANSITION_REQUIREMENTS`, keyed by the era being LEFT), reusing three systems that already exist rather than inventing new state to track.** A settlement is ready to leave Tribal once: population ≥ 15 (the second Dunbar-inspired threshold the Tribal info panel already cites, and the same number as `log.POPULATION_MILESTONES[1]`); the late Tribal research tier is *unlocked* (not necessarily finished — the same soft-gate spirit `research.tier_unlocked()` already uses); and the sustainability score is at least "Strained" on the same five-band scale the UI already narrates with. No new save fields, no new bookkeeping — `transition_ready()`/`missing_requirements()` are pure functions of `(state, tree, effects)`, the same convention `sustainability.py` and `research.py` already follow, so a "why can't I move on yet" explanation is exactly as testable as a locked research node's reason already is.
- **This surfaced a genuine design gap in the doc, resolved as a documented judgement call rather than a blocker**: the design doc never states what makes a settlement "done" with an era — it describes eras narratively (era 2 is "settled agriculture, early surplus and trade") but gives no numeric or mechanical threshold for crossing into one. The three-part requirement above is that judgement call, made the same way Phase 1's own open calls were (equal component weighting, the equity-spread penalty, population-milestone numbers) — reusing existing systems rather than adding a fourth one, and documented here rather than silently assumed. Worth revisiting once more than one era transition exists and a pattern (or a reason to vary it per era) becomes clearer.
- **`sustainability.SCORE_LABELS` is now a public, ordered constant** (`["Collapsing", "Failing", "Strained", "Steady", "Thriving"]`), refactored out of `score_label()`'s own if/elif chain rather than added alongside it — same return values, zero behaviour change (all 139 tests, including every pre-existing `test_sustainability.py` assertion on exact label strings, passed unchanged). `log.py`'s own `_LABEL_ORDER` now aliases this instead of keeping a second hand-copied list, closing the exact class of bug the Milestone 6 build notes flagged (a hand-copied, wrongly-cased label list silently breaking save/restore) at its root rather than only in the one place it was first caught.
- **`Chronicle.log_transition()` is the one new public method on `log.Chronicle`**, added specifically so `transition.py` has a real API to call rather than reaching into `Chronicle._add()` from outside its own module. `check_research()`/`check_population()`/`check_livability()` stay `Chronicle`'s own triggers; a transition beat is decided by `transition.py`'s own readiness rules instead, so it gets its own narrow entry point.
- **A transition beat is a full, longer paragraph, not another one-line `log.py` entry** — the doc's "bigger narrative moment" language, honoured by giving `TRANSITION_BEATS` its own table (keyed by `(from_era, to_era)`, with a generic era-name fallback for a pair with no bespoke prose yet) rather than reusing `log.py`'s short-line tables. It still rides through `Chronicle` as an ordinary entry with `kind="transition"`, so the render path Milestone 6 already built needs no changes to display one once Milestone 8 wires this in — confirmed by giving `.log-row--transition` its own CSS accent now (a distinct violet, unused today) so that wiring is a pure content change, not a styling one.
- **`attempt_transition()` refuses cleanly, every time, rather than ever raising** — not ready, no transition defined from the current era (including the true end of the arc), or a revisit in progress (delegated straight to `Campaign.advance_to_era()`'s own guard, not re-implemented here) all return `False` with no side effects, tested individually. This matters more here than for most refusal paths in this codebase: a transition is meant to be a rare, weighty, once-per-era action, so a silent no-op on a race or a stale button click is far preferable to a half-applied era change.
- **Verified live under Pyodide directly** (not just the fake-DOM suite, since there is no DOM path to exercise yet): fetched `transition.py` into a running page's virtual filesystem by hand, built a second `Campaign`, pushed it past all three Tribal→Agrarian requirements, and called `attempt_transition()` — it correctly returned `True`, moved `state.era` to `"agrarian"`, and logged the exact `TRANSITION_BEATS[("tribal", "agrarian")]` text, under the real interpreter this game ships on rather than only under CPython/pytest. Test count: 123 → 139 (16 new tests in `tests/test_transition_system.py`).

## Milestone 8 build decisions (first full transition: Tribal → Agrarian)

The biggest, most judgement-heavy milestone so far — concrete calls made building it, grounded in the doc's "settled agriculture, early surplus and trade" framing and in patterns Phase 1/Milestones 6–7 already established.

- **Agrarian's new content: one role (Farmers), one building (Farmland), one resource (`surplus`) — no separate "trader" role.** The doc's own framing is "settled agriculture, early surplus and trade" as one continuous idea, not two mechanics bolted together, so trade is folded into the existing spoilage step (below) rather than given its own worker role. This kept the role count at five instead of six and avoided a second, parallel resource-conversion loop competing with the one the season loop already has.
- **Roles and buildings are cumulative across eras, not swapped per era — confirmed as the intended model, not just chosen.** Phase 1's own comment on `sim.ROLES` already said "later eras extend the list rather than replacing the mechanic," and the Milestone 4 audit's `CITY_KEYED_DICTS` fix was built specifically to tolerate a save gaining a role/building/resource key it didn't have before. Milestone 8 is the first era to actually exercise that: `sim.ERA_ROLES`/`sim.ERA_BUILDINGS` are dicts of *additions* per era, `sim.roles_for_era(era)`/`buildings_for_era(era)` accumulate everything unlocked by that era or an earlier one, and the flat `sim.ROLES`/`sim.BUILDINGS` lists (used by allocation/buildings dicts, `clamp_allocation()`, and save.py's key-by-key restore) are simply every key across every era table. A Tribal settlement still has a `"farmers"` key sitting at 0 in its allocation dict from the moment it's created — it just can't be assigned to until the era unlocks it.
- **`FOOD_PER_FARMER` (5.0) is noticeably higher than `FOOD_PER_FORAGER` (3.0)**, and `FARMLAND_YIELD_BONUS` (0.4 per field) mirrors `TOOLWORKS_CRAFT_BONUS`'s (0.3) role exactly — a building that multiplies one role's output, the same established shape. Farmers still scale through the *same* `tool_factor`/`land_health`/`food_yield_mult` chain foragers do, and their harvest feeds the *same* shared `extraction` total the land-health system already tracks (see `sim.CityState.advance_season()`'s harvest step) — deliberately: agriculture is more productive here, not a second economy exempt from what the land can sustain. This is what keeps the core sustainability mechanic (a small well-run settlement can outscore a careless big one) meaning the same thing in Agrarian as it did in Tribal, rather than needing a second version of the land model.
- **`role_diversity()` now measures against `sim.roles_for_era(self.era)`, not the flat `ROLES` list — a real bug caught before it shipped, not a hypothetical one.** Adding a `"farmers"` key to the allocation dict silently changed `role_diversity()`'s denominator (`even = 1/len(ROLES)`) for every *Tribal* settlement too, since the dict now had five keys instead of four even while a Tribal city couldn't assign a single worker to the fifth. Every Milestone 1/2 test asserting exact resilience numbers caught this immediately once `sim.CityState()` started including `"farmers": 0`. Scoping the diversity calculation to the current era's own unlocked roles keeps every pre-Milestone-8 Tribal sustainability number byte-identical, and is what makes the "roles are cumulative, not swapped" model safe to extend into five more eras without re-litigating every earlier era's numbers each time.
- **`surplus` is fed by the *existing* spoilage step, not a new production formula.** Per the doc's own Agrarian sources (National Geographic/HISTORY on storable surplus; EBSCO on surplus enabling specialization), once the settlement has reached Agrarian, a fraction of what would have spoiled past storage capacity (`sim.SURPLUS_CONVERSION_RATE`, 0.4, boosted by the new `surplus_conversion_bonus` effect key) is banked as `surplus` instead of pure waste — the rest still spoils exactly as before. This is deliberately a *reinterpretation* of an already-existing number (what would spoil) rather than a new produced-by-role quantity, which is why it needed zero new production math and slotted into one `if` inside step 6 of the season loop. Tribal-era behaviour is provably unchanged: the era-gate means `surplus_banked` is always `0.0` before Agrarian, so `spoiled` and the `food` deduction are bit-for-bit what they were before this milestone.
- **One new effects key (`surplus_conversion_bonus`), added to `NEUTRAL_EFFECTS` rather than opening a second channel.** Still exactly one seam — `tree.effects()` → `advance_season(effects)` — just one more key in the same dict, the same way Tribal's own 11 keys already cover a dozen unrelated mechanics. `Market Custom` (the one Agrarian node that grants it) is the only node that touches it.
- **The sustainability adjustment the doc calls for ("what livable means shifts somewhat per era") is a new equity penalty for hoarded surplus, era-gated and ratio-based.** `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s TxWes Pressbooks source is explicit that surplus is what created the first social hierarchies and property accumulation — `sustainability._surplus_hoarding_penalty()` makes that mechanically real: surplus-per-capita above `SURPLUS_FAIR_SHARE` (20.0) costs up to `SURPLUS_HOARDING_PENALTY_WEIGHT` (0.3) off the equity component, for any era at or after Agrarian. Per-capita, not raw — doubling population and surplus together leaves the penalty unchanged, tested directly, the same ratio-based rule the doc's own "small tribal settlement beats a poorly-run industrial one" requirement holds every other input in this file to. Zero effect before Agrarian by construction (an explicit era-index guard), so it doesn't retroactively change any Tribal-era score.
- **Six new research nodes (tiers 3–4, one per branch per tier)**, each chaining a prerequisite back into a specific Tribal tier-2 node (`plow_and_furrow`←`stone_knapping`, `seed_selection`←`seasonal_rounds`, `communal_granaries`←`kinship_custom`) — proof that "early choices echo later" holds *across* an era boundary, not just within Tribal's own two tiers the way `elders_council` already proved. `market_custom` reuses the same `min_affinity` mechanism `elders_council` introduced (now requiring 3 community-branch discoveries, reachable via any mix of Tribal's three community nodes plus `communal_granaries`), so a settlement that leaned into community early unlocks it more easily than one that didn't — the same emphasis-carries-forward result, one era later. All six reuse existing effect keys except `market_custom`'s `surplus_conversion_bonus`.
- **The Work and Build panels became dynamically rendered, not a bolt-on for Farmers/Farmland specifically.** `sim.py`'s own Phase 1 comment flagged this moment explicitly: "[the label/blurb tables were] dead until the moment a second era ships." `render_work()`/`render_buildings()` now rebuild every row from `sim.roles_for_era(state.era)`/`buildings_for_era(state.era)` each render — the exact same shape `render_research()` already used for the same reason (a growing roster can't stay static markup) — including the same create_proxy-per-row tracking/destroy discipline the Milestone 4 audit already required for research buttons (`_work_button_proxies`/`_building_button_proxies`, tested directly against a repeated render, plus a test confirming a role gained mid-session via a real transition gets a live, non-leaked proxy). The emoji prefix, previously static markup per Phase 1's own explicit note ("the emoji prefix... stays static markup — it's decoration, not data"), necessarily moved into `sim.ROLE_EMOJI`/`BUILDING_EMOJI` now that rows are generated in Python — a small, documented reversal of that one Phase 1 decision, forced by the same architectural shift research row rendering already went through.
- **The Era Progress UI section is what actually exposes `transition.py` to a player**, per Milestone 7's own scope note that this was deliberately deferred until Agrarian had real content. It shows the next era, whether the settlement is ready, and — reusing `transition.missing_requirements()` verbatim — why not if it isn't, the same "one description of why not, shared by the UI and the tests" pattern the research panel's locked-node reasons already use. The button calls `transition.attempt_transition(campaign)` and only re-renders on success; a stray click after a transition already happened is a no-op (tested), since `attempt_transition()` itself refuses cleanly once `next_era_for()` has nothing to return.
- **No genuine blocker hit.** The one open design question this milestone depended on — "what makes a settlement ready to leave an era" — was already identified and resolved as a documented judgement call in Milestone 7's own build notes (population + research-tier unlock + sustainability-score band), not something this milestone needed to re-litigate; Milestone 8 only had to build content grounded enough to make that judgement call exercise meaningfully for real.
- **Verified live under Pyodide end to end**, through the actual shipped UI rather than by calling engine functions by hand: booted the page, confirmed the Era Progress panel correctly reports "Working toward the Agrarian era" with both missing-requirement reasons listed and the button disabled; pushed the settlement past all three requirements and confirmed the button enabled with the correct reason text clearing; clicked the real "Advance to Agrarian" button and confirmed in one pass — era flipped to `"agrarian"`, Farmers and Farmland rows appeared in the Work/Build panels with correct label/blurb text, the Log gained exactly one `transition`-kind entry matching `TRANSITION_BEATS[("tribal", "agrarian")]`, the Era Progress panel now reads "Nothing more to reach from here yet," and the info panel (once opened) showed the real Agrarian sources instead of Tribal's. Also round-tripped a save carrying live Farmers/Farmland/`surplus` values through the actual `get_state()` → `json.dumps`/`json.loads` → `load_state()` path into a *second*, independent Campaign object (not just reloading into the same one) and confirmed every Agrarian-specific field survived. Advancing a season after the transition correctly banked surplus and narrated it ("1 preserved as trade surplus") alongside the existing land-pressure warning. Only console output throughout was the pre-existing service-worker-registration error common to every hub game in the preview sandbox. Test count: 139 → 169 (30 new/updated tests: `tests/test_agrarian_era.py`, plus one new Agrarian-tier-shape test in `tests/test_research.py` and small backward-compatibility fixes to three pre-existing tests in `test_core_loop.py`/`test_sustainability.py`/`test_save_system.py` whose literal role/resource-set lists needed the new keys added — same category of update Milestone 6 already made once to `test_save_system.py`'s schema-key set).

## Milestone 9 build decisions (Classical era content expansion)

The second full era transition, and the first real test of whether Milestone 8's patterns generalise or were built to fit exactly one case. Grounded in `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s Classical section (Uruk/Sumer: temple-directed irrigation surplus funding centralized infrastructure, canal management requiring coordinated labor and planning, city populations in the tens of thousands, trade networks spanning hundreds of miles).

- **Classical's new content: one role (Administrators), one building (Canals) — but with a two-way dependency Milestone 8's Farmers/Farmland pairing didn't have.** Farmland always boosts Farmers, full stop — the building simply multiplies the role's output. Canals are different on purpose: a Canal's yield bonus is scaled by how fully Administrators staff it (`min(1.0, administrators / (canals * ADMINISTRATORS_PER_CANAL))`), and a built-but-unstaffed canal contributes *nothing*. This is a direct, literal read of TheCollector's source ("canal management required centralized planning and coordinated labor") — the infrastructure alone was never the point, the coordinated labor running it was. Administrators produce no resource of their own; their entire purpose is staffing canals, which is itself the lesson (a civic-institution role that does nothing until there's civic infrastructure to run) rather than an oversight.
- **`CANAL_YIELD_BONUS` (0.5 per fully-staffed canal) is deliberately larger than `FARMLAND_YIELD_BONUS` (0.4)**, reflecting the sources' own framing that coordinated irrigation at city scale (Uruk's ~45,000–100,000 population per the Getty/GlobalSecurity sources) was a bigger productivity jump than farmland alone, not just a second copy of the same bonus. `ADMINISTRATORS_PER_CANAL` (2) sets how many administrators fully staff one canal; the ratio is capped at 1.0, so over-staffing a canal wastes labor rather than compounding the bonus (tested directly — `test_canal_staffing_ratio_is_capped_at_one_extra_administrators_dont_help_further`).
- **A new effects key, `canal_yield_bonus`**, additive onto `CANAL_YIELD_BONUS` the same way Milestone 8's `surplus_conversion_bonus` sat additive onto `SURPLUS_CONVERSION_RATE` — still exactly one seam (`tree.effects()` → `advance_season(effects)`), one more key in the same dict.
- **The season report gained two new fields (`canals`, `canal_staffing_ratio`) purely for narration.** `game.py`'s `season_report_message()` now calls out an under-staffed canal by name ("Canals running at 0% coordination — more administrators needed") the same way it already calls out spoilage or land overuse — mechanically teaching the "coordinated labor, not just infrastructure" lesson through the log rather than a tooltip, per the design doc's whole "the player discovers it through play" philosophy. Verified live: building a canal with zero administrators assigned and advancing a season produced exactly this line.
- **The sustainability adjustment is a NEW tension, not a reuse of Milestone 8's equity-hoarding shape** — a deliberate judgement call per the task's own instruction not to force a parallel where the sources don't support one. The sources describe coordinated labor as a genuine cost/tradeoff (administration is real labor, not a free multiplier), not a hierarchy-and-hoarding story the way Agrarian's surplus was. So this is a **resilience** penalty (`_administrative_overextension_penalty()`), not an equity one: once a settlement's Administrators exceed `ADMIN_SHARE_FAIR_LIMIT` (20%) of its *assigned workforce* (a ratio, not a headcount — doubling population and administrators together leaves the penalty unchanged, tested directly), it costs up to `ADMIN_OVEREXTENSION_PENALTY_WEIGHT` (0.25) off resilience. The reasoning: a settlement that pours a large share of its workforce into coordination beyond what its canals actually need staffed isn't investing in infrastructure, it's hollowing out its own productive capacity — exactly the kind of top-heavy arrangement with nothing to fall back on that the resilience component ("could it survive a bad season?") already exists to catch. Era-gated at Classical+ by construction (an explicit era-index guard, zero effect on any Agrarian-or-earlier `resilience()` result).
- **A genuine wrinkle found while testing the overextension penalty, worth recording**: comparing a "top-heavy" settlement (half its workforce in Administrators) against an all-foragers settlement showed the *top-heavy one scoring higher* on resilience despite the penalty — because `role_diversity()` (an existing, unrelated Phase 1 mechanic) rewards spreading across more roles far more heavily than the new penalty costs, and moving workers into a fifth/sixth role is itself a big diversity gain. This isn't a bug in the new penalty — it's a real interaction between two mechanics that both reward/punish allocation shape. The comparison that actually isolates the penalty (and the one committed to the test suite, `test_a_top_heavy_administration_costs_resilience_from_classical_on`) holds the workforce split fixed at 50/50 across exactly two roles and only changes *which* second role — Administrators vs. Gatherers — which keeps `role_diversity()` byte-identical between the two and shows the penalty's effect in isolation. Worth remembering for any future era that adds both a new role and a resilience-side penalty: `role_diversity()`'s reward for spreading out can swamp a new penalty in a naive head-to-head unless the comparison controls for it.
- **Six new research nodes (tiers 5–6, one per branch per tier)**, continuing the exact chain-back pattern Milestone 8 established: each Classical early-tier (5) node chains a prerequisite into the *same branch's* late-Agrarian (tier 4) node — `canal_engineering`←`irrigation_channels` (craft), `managed_irrigation`←`crop_rotation` (provision), `temple_administration`←`market_custom` (community) — and each late-tier (6) node chains into its own era's early-tier node. Every one of the three branches (provision/community/craft) now has an unbroken prerequisite chain three eras deep (Tribal → Agrarian → Classical), not just two. `civic_assembly` reuses the `min_affinity` mechanism `elders_council`/`market_custom` already established, requiring 4 community-branch discoveries — one more than `market_custom`'s 3, which itself was one more than `elders_council`'s 2 — so the "early emphasis keeps echoing" effect visibly compounds across three eras rather than being proven once and left flat. `trade_networks` deliberately reuses the existing `surplus_conversion_bonus` effect key (grounded in GlobalSecurity's trade-network source) rather than inventing a new one, the same "reuse an existing seam" discipline the sim-side canal bonus also follows.
- **The Agrarian→Classical transition requirement reuses Milestone 7's exact three-part shape verbatim** (population threshold / research-tier-unlocked / minimum sustainability-score band) rather than re-litigating it — Milestone 7's own build notes flagged this as "worth revisiting once more than one era transition exists and a pattern... becomes clearer," and with a second data point in hand the shape held; only the specific numbers needed choosing. **Population 25** is `log.POPULATION_MILESTONES[2]`, the next Dunbar-ish layer up from Tribal→Agrarian's 15, and lines up with that milestone's own text ("outgrown a size a handful of people can hold together by memory alone") — a fitting real-world hinge for exactly the "kinship-scale coordination gives way to administered institutions" shift this era's sources describe. **The minimum score band was kept at "Strained"**, the same bar as Tribal→Agrarian, rather than raised — a judgement call in favor of consistency over inventing a reason to diverge; nothing in the sources argues Classical specifically demands a *more* stable settlement to begin, just a *bigger* and *more coordinated* one, which the population and research requirements already capture.

## Milestone 10 build decisions (Medieval era content expansion)

The third full era transition, and the point where the sources themselves argued against just repeating Milestone 9's shape a second time. Grounded in `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s Medieval section (Popular Archaeology on real sanitation regulation and toxic-trade zoning; SAGE's Coomans & Hermenault on Ghent's public-works spending as risk mitigation; JHU Press's Magnusson, literally titled around sustainability and resilience in medieval public works; Fiveable on guild specialisation and municipal self-governance).

- **Medieval's new content: one role (Guildmasters), one building (Public Works) — but Public Works is deliberately NOT staffed, breaking the Canals/Administrators labor-dependency pattern on purpose.** Canals needed Administrators to deliver any bonus at all because the Classical sources were specifically about *coordinated labor* running shared infrastructure. Medieval's public-works sources (SAGE, JHU Press) are about something different: money and labor spent *ahead of* a shock that might not come, purely as a hedge against flood, disease and military vulnerability — there is no "coordination" story in these sources for a staffing mechanic to dramatise. Forcing Canals' two-way dependency onto Public Works would have been fitting Milestone 9's shape onto a source that doesn't support it, which the task explicitly warned against. So Public Works has no worker requirement at all: `sim.CityState.public_works_coverage()` is a pure function of building count (`buildings * PUBLIC_WORKS_COVERAGE_PER_BUILDING * (1 + effects["public_works_bonus"])`), read only by `sustainability.py`'s `resilience()` — never by anything in the season loop's own production math. Guildmasters, by contrast, follow Milestone 8/9's established "new role feeds the existing shared economy" shape exactly: they produce tools at `TOOLS_PER_GUILDMASTER` (1.3, vs. Crafters' 0.8 — roughly the same ~1.6x "trained specialist" bump `FOOD_PER_FARMER` gave Farmers over Foragers), through the *same* Knapping Site bonus and `tool_yield_mult` chain Crafters already use — one tool economy, not two.

Medieval's public-works sources describe no equivalent imbalance to punish — investing in flood/disease/fire mitigation ahead of a bad season is simply, unconditionally a good hedge; there's nothing to be "too much" of in the way surplus or administrators could be. Because this mechanic is purely building-based and never touches `allocation`, it has **no interaction with `role_diversity()` to guard against** — unlike Classical's overextension penalty (see Milestone 9's build notes on that exact trap), there's no allocation-shape confound possible here, which is itself worth recording so a future era doesn't have to rediscover why this one particular mechanic was safe to test more simply. ## Milestone 11 build decisions (Industrial era content expansion)

The fourth full era transition, and the first one the task itself flagged as needing to be structurally different from the last three — CLAUDE.md's own "Eras (confirmed)" list calls Industrial "the era where sustainability tension becomes most visible historically," and the task explicitly warned against defaulting to another simple additive score-side bonus/penalty the way Agrarian/Classical/Medieval's adjustments all were. Grounded in `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s Industrial section (Britannica on industrial growth outpacing planning and producing slums/disease before sanitation reform; the Economic Journal/Oxford Academic study showing industrial pollution *measurably reduced long-run city growth*; Sociology.Institute's 3%→50% urbanization statistic; PMC on modern urban planning emerging from industrial-era public health crises).

- **The central design call: `pollution` is a real in-sim stock with a GROWTH-side consequence, not only a score-side one — this is what makes Industrial mechanically different from every prior era.** Agrarian's surplus-hoarding penalty, Classical's overextension penalty and Medieval's public-works bonus all worked purely through `sustainability.py`; none of them touched the simulation's own growth math. The Economic Journal source gave a specific, well-grounded basis to do otherwise: pollution had a *real, provable* growth cost historically, not just a moral one. So `sim.CityState.pollution` (a new field, 0..1) is read directly by `advance_season()`'s own population-growth step — `POLLUTION_GROWTH_PENALTY_WEIGHT` (0.6) scales `GROWTH_RATE` down proportionally to however polluted the settlement currently is, before growth_progress accumulates at all. `sustainability._industrial_pollution_penalty()` is a separate, smaller function that additionally docks the *resource balance* score component — the two are deliberately independent effects of the same one stock, not the same mechanism counted twice.
- **Pollution is modelled as a lagged stock with the exact same shape `land_health` already uses, on purpose — reuse over invention.** Rather than inventing a new causality model, `state.pollution` is produced by Factory Workers, absorbed by Sanitation Works, decays a little on its own every season (so an idle-but-already-polluted settlement isn't stuck forever), and is updated in the season loop's step 8b — right after `land_health`'s own update, and for the same reason: this season's growth check (step 7) reads the pollution value as it stood at the *end of the previous season*, not anything this season's own production computes. This mirrors exactly how a season of land overuse doesn't tank *that* season's growth, only the next one's. Documented as a deliberate structural echo, not a coincidence — a future era's own environmental mechanic (if one is ever needed) has a proven shape to reuse rather than inventing a third one.
- **The growth penalty is deliberately a SOFT, proportional slowdown, not `land_health`'s HARD gate.** `GROWTH_MIN_LAND_HEALTH` blocks growth outright below a threshold; pollution instead scales `GROWTH_RATE` down continuously via `(1.0 - pollution * POLLUTION_GROWTH_PENALTY_WEIGHT)`, capping at a 60% reduction when fully polluted rather than ever stopping growth dead. This is a direct, literal read of the Economic Journal source's own language — "measurably *reduced*" growth, not "halted" it — and gives the mechanic a genuinely different shape from land health's, rather than being a reskinned copy of the same gate.
- **Factory Workers feed the existing shared materials economy, the same discipline every prior era's new production role has been held to.** `MATERIALS_PER_FACTORY_WORKER` (5.0) is a noticeably bigger jump over `MATERIALS_PER_GATHERER` (2.0) than any previous era's role addition was over its predecessor — Farmers over Foragers and Guildmasters over Crafters were both roughly a ~1.6x bump (documented in Milestones 8 and 10's own build notes), but mechanization was a categorically larger real-world productivity leap than any prior era's shift, per the sources' own framing of "rapid growth" — so the ratio here (2.5x) is deliberately bigger, a documented judgement call rather than a repeat of the earlier ratio just because it worked twice before. Factory Workers still route through the same `tool_factor`/`land_health`/`materials_yield_mult` chain Gatherers do, and feed the same shared `extraction` total — one materials economy, not two.
- **Sanitation Works is unstaffed, like Public Works — but unlike Public Works, it DOES interact with the season loop, and that divergence is deliberate, not an inconsistency.** Public Works (Milestone 10) was built specifically to be read *only* by `sustainability.py`, never by the season loop's own production math — Medieval's sources described an unconditional hedge with nothing for a staffing mechanic to dramatize. Sanitation Works reuses that same "built, not staffed" shape (the Britannica source frames sanitation reform as public infrastructure investment, not a coordinated-labor story the way Classical's canals were), but this era's whole point requires *something* in the simulation itself to respond to sanitation investment — so Sanitation Works absorbs pollution directly inside `advance_season()`, the first building in the game to be both "unstaffed" (Public Works' shape) and "read by production math" (Canals' shape) at once. Worth flagging explicitly so a future era doesn't read "unstaffed" and "doesn't touch the season loop" as the same rule — they're two independent choices that happened to both be true for Public Works, not one.
- **The sustainability adjustment lives in `balance()`, not `equity()` or `resilience()` — a genuine judgement call, not the only defensible option.** Britannica's framing (slums, disease, growth outpacing planning) could plausibly have been read as an equity or livability question instead. `balance()` was chosen because pollution and land overuse are the same *kind* of problem mechanically — both are "the settlement taking more from its surroundings than it puts back" — and because equity/resilience already carry Agrarian's and Classical/Medieval's own adjustments respectively; spreading Industrial's new mechanic across yet another component risked diluting what each component is legible as "the one thing that tracks X." `INDUSTRIAL_POLLUTION_PENALTY_WEIGHT` (0.3) matches Agrarian's hoarding-penalty weight exactly, a consistency call rather than a finding.
- **No extra ratio work needed for the score-side penalty, unlike every prior era-specific adjustment in this file.** Agrarian's hoarding penalty needed surplus-per-capita, Classical's overextension penalty needed administrators' *share* of the workforce, Medieval's bonus needed coverage-per-capita — all of them had to turn a raw count into a ratio by hand to stay scale-neutral. `state.pollution` is already a 0..1 ratio-like stock by construction (see its own build-up/decay model above), so `_industrial_pollution_penalty()` is the first era-specific adjustment in the file that doesn't need to divide by population at all — tested directly (`test_industrial_pollution_penalty_does_not_need_a_ratio_the_other_adjustments_needed`).
- **Interaction check, per the task's explicit instruction to watch for this the way Milestone 9 did for `role_diversity()` and Milestone 10 confirmed as a non-issue for its building-only mechanic**: Factory Workers *is* a new allocation-dict key, unlike Milestone 10's building-only Public Works, so it interacts with `role_diversity()` the same way Farmers/Administrators/Guildmasters all did before it. ## Milestone 13 build decisions (Space Age era content expansion — Phase 3 complete)

The sixth and final full era transition, and the only genuinely speculative era in the whole arc — CLAUDE.md's own "Eras (confirmed)" list calls it "future era, off-world or post-scarcity infrastructure questions," unlike the six historical/present-day eras before it. Grounded in `continuum-real-world-sources.md`'s Space Age section: Big Think's survey of three real off-world design concepts (NASA's 1977 rotating-habitat study, MIT's Mars City "Redwood Forest" competition winner, ring habitats); Planetizen's framing of NASA's 1977 "Space Settlements: A Design Study" as literally an urban-planning policy document; Columbia University Press's academic history (Scharmen) treating those 1975 Summer Study designs as serious architecture; and the current (2025) Dagstuhl/SpaceCHI research on modular space habitat layout usability — the one source here that isn't 1970s-era material.

- **The central design call, and the biggest open question of the whole Phase 3 arc: Space Age's mechanic is deliberately NOT a third growth-side lagged stock, the way it would have been if Industrial's pollution and Digital's sprawl had simply been extended a third time.** The task itself framed three real candidates from the sources — (a) a closed-loop/life-support resource constraint, echoing the land_health/extraction lineage's natural endpoint now that there's no more "land" off-world; (b) a modular-habitat-layout/usability mechanic per the Dagstuhl source, about layout efficiency rather than growth or pollution; (c) whether "post-scarcity" should mean this era's tension is fundamentally about something other than resource scarcity — coordination/usability/psychological livability instead. The four actual sources support (b) directly (the Dagstuhl paper is literally about modular layout usability "at scale") and (c) indirectly (Planetizen's framing of space settlement design as continuous with *ordinary* urban planning argues against treating "off-world" as a total departure requiring a whole new scarcity mechanic) — but none of the four sources are actually about closed-loop life-support resource scarcity as their own subject, so (a) was demoted from "a second mechanical lever" to narrative/research-content grounding only (see the extraction_efficiency callback below), rather than forced into a redundant third stock just because the task raised it as a candidate. Building a third copy of pollution/sprawl's shape here is exactly the "seventh copy of the same growth-vs-livability shape" the task explicitly warned against defaulting to.

This is the first era-specific mechanic in the whole game to live inside `provisions()`'s own shared plumbing rather than as a bolt-on `_xxx_penalty()`/`_xxx_bonus()` function beside an existing component the way Agrarian through Digital's five adjustments all were. It is also the first time `livability` itself (as opposed to equity/balance/resilience) carries an era-specific mechanic at all — a deliberate choice: `livability` had gone all six prior eras with zero era-specific content, and the Dagstuhl source's whole point (usability, not scarcity or resilience) is a genuinely better fit for what `livability` already measures ("are people fed, housed, and part of something?") than for any of the other three components.
- **The building/role relationship deliberately reuses Classical's Canal/Administrator shape (a building whose bonus is scaled by how fully a role staffs it, capped at 1.0) rather than Medieval/Industrial/Digital's "unstaffed building" shape (Public Works/Sanitation Works/Transit Hubs).** `CityState.habitat_capacity()` computes `layout_quality = min(1.0, architects / (habitat_rings * ARCHITECTS_PER_RING))`, the exact same formula shape `canal_staffing` used seven tiers ago — a deliberate structural echo, not a coincidence: the Dagstuhl source's whole point is that a modular structure's raw capacity and its actual, lived usability are two different numbers, which is precisely the lesson Classical's canals taught about coordinated labor and irrigation infrastructure, now applied to coordinated *design* instead of coordinated *labor*. A Habitat Ring built with zero Architects assigned still physically holds people (like an unstaffed canal is still a ditch) but serves none of them well.
- **A deliberate asymmetry worth recording, since it's genuinely different from how every prior era's mechanic onset: `habitat_usability()` starts at its WORST reading (0.0) the instant Space Age is reached, not at its best.** Every prior era's lagged-stock mechanic (pollution, sprawl) starts at 0.0 meaning "no problem yet" and worsens with neglect; `habitat_usability()` starts at 0.0 meaning "no rings built yet, so nobody is well-served" and only improves with investment, because `habitat_rings` can't be built before Space Age is actually reached (the same era-gating every building has always had) — there is no way to prepare in advance the way, say, building extra Shelter ahead of a population boom is possible with ordinary housing. Confirmed non-catastrophic (not a score-breaking cliff) since only one of four equally-weighted components takes the direct hit, verified by hand during the season-loop check above rather than a dedicated test asserting a specific number (the exact severity is a tuning question, not a correctness one — see the constants note below).

## Phase 4 build decisions (save/security audit)

Named "Phase 4 build decisions" rather than "Milestone 14," matching the file's own convention: Phases 1-3 broke their work into numbered milestones with table rows because each one shipped separable content; Phase 4's own scope description (above) was always a single, atomic "dedicated audit pass," never subdivided into a table the way Phase 1-3 were, so it gets a phase-level section the same way "Phase 1 build decisions" already covers Milestones 1-4 collectively. Tagged `continuum-phase-4-audit`.

This pass did exactly what CLAUDE.md's own Phase 4 description asked for: read `save.py` end to end, then spent most of its time hand-loading adversarial and malformed save dicts into a real `Campaign` (both directly, and through `game.py`'s actual `get_state()`/`load_state()` widget contract via the pytest fake-DOM harness) and watching what broke. Six real era transitions now exist, so the revisit system finally has genuine traffic to audit against — this is the first point CLAUDE.md's own Phase 4 note said the pass would be meaningful.

**Real issues found and fixed** — six genuine bugs, not manufactured busywork:

- **An unrecognised `era` string crashed the next `sim.era_index()` call, and was reachable two different ways.** `restore_city()` used to write `data["era"]` straight into `state.era` (and, from there, into `tree.current_era`) with zero validation — only the top-level `current_era` key got the `if era in sim.ERA_ORDER` check `load_dict()` already had. A save whose *nested* `current_state.city.era` was a bogus string (with no valid top-level `current_era` to override it) loaded "successfully" and then raised `ValueError` on the very next season. Worse, because `restore_city()` is the same function every revisit path uses, a hand-edited `era_snapshots` entry with a bad nested `era` field crashed the instant a player revisited it — reproduced directly, not hypothesized: `campaign.enter_revisit("tribal")` against a snapshot whose `city.era` read `"not_a_real_era"` returned `True` and then blew up on the next `advance_season()`. Fixed by validating `era` against `sim.ERA_ORDER` inside `restore_city()` itself, the same way every other cross-checked field in this schema already is — an invalid value is now treated exactly like a missing one (kept at whatever it already was).
- **A whole family of adversarial values in `resources`/`allocation`/`buildings`/scalar fields loaded "successfully" and then crashed or corrupted state on the next season or render** — the exact "widget already reported success" failure shape the original Milestone 4 audit's `CITY_KEYED_DICTS` fix was written to close for a *different* problem (a save with fewer keys than the build). This pass found the mirror problem: a save with the *right* keys but *wrong values*. Confirmed by hand-loading each of these into a real `Campaign` before fixing anything: `population: "not-a-number"` and any keyed-dict value of `null` (`resources.food`, `allocation.foragers`, `buildings.shelter`) all crashed with a `TypeError` on the very next `advance_season()`; `population: -50`, `resources.food: -999999.0`, and `buildings.shelter: -5` all loaded without crashing but left the settlement in a nonsensical state (negative population, negative food); `land_health: -50.0` fed directly into that *same* season's harvest math before the season loop's own end-of-season clamp ever ran. Fixed with a general sanitizer in `restore_city()` (`_is_finite_number()` / `_sanitize_numeric_field()`): every `CITY_FIELDS` scalar and every `CITY_KEYED_DICTS` value is now type- and range-checked before it's trusted, with an invalid value treated as absent (kept at its current value) rather than written through. Pinned by six tests in `tests/test_save_system.py`, each asserting the *specific* restored value (e.g. `population == sim.MIN_POPULATION`, not just "didn't crash").
- **Python's own `json.loads` accepts the non-standard `NaN`/`Infinity`/`-Infinity` tokens** (a genuine gap between what Python's stdlib parses and what the JSON spec — and stricter parsers elsewhere in the hub's own stack — actually allow), and a bare `max(low, min(high, value))` clamp silently returns a finite number for a NaN input *depending on argument order* rather than rejecting it, which is exactly the kind of "looks handled" trap this pass was looking for. A NaN resource value used to propagate forever (`x + nan` is always `nan`), permanently corrupting that resource, and — since `json.dumps` round-trips a Python NaN back out as the literal token `NaN` — writing back a save a stricter JSON parser elsewhere in the hub's stack could refuse to read at all. `_is_finite_number()` (used by the same sanitizer above) explicitly checks `math.isfinite()`, so a NaN/±inf value is now refused rather than silently clamped by accident.
- **A hand-edited `growth_progress` could turn `advance_season()`'s own population-growth loop into a genuine, reproducible page-freeze.** That loop (`while self.growth_progress >= 1.0 and self.population < housing: ...`) consumes exactly 1.0 of progress per iteration; in legitimate play this value never strays far above 1.0 (the loop itself drains any excess the moment there's housing room), but nothing enforced that on load. Reproduced directly: loading `growth_progress: 200000.0` and calling `advance_season()` iterated 200,000 times before returning — trivial in CPython, but Pyodide typically runs several times slower, and a save with a genuinely large value (`10**9`, say) would have frozen a player's browser tab for a long time, not merely produced a bad number. Fixed with `save.GROWTH_PROGRESS_MAX` (10.0, generous headroom above anything legitimate play produces) as this field's upper bound in the same sanitizer.
- **`enter_revisit()`/`exit_revisit()` could be pointed at the wrong era by a tampered snapshot, decoupling `campaign.revisiting` from `state.era`.** `era_snapshots` and `parked_state` both ride in the save payload; each snapshot's own nested `city.era` field is *supposed* to always agree with the era it's keyed/parked under (that's what `city_snapshot()` always records in ordinary play), but nothing cross-checked that a hand-edited save couldn't disagree with itself. Before the `restore_city()` fix above, a mismatched or invalid nested `era` field either redirected `state.era` somewhere the player didn't ask for, or (after that fix) simply left `state.era` at whatever it was *before* the call — either way, `campaign.revisiting`/`furthest_era` and the live `state.era` could end up describing two different eras at once, the literal "parked state and actual progress cross-contaminate" failure mode this audit was specifically asked to check for. Fixed by having `enter_revisit(era)` force `state.era = era` (the argument, now validated against `sim.ERA_ORDER` as well as `era_snapshots` — belt-and-suspenders against a bogus snapshot *key* too, not just a bogus nested field) and `exit_revisit()` force `state.era = self.furthest_era` (the schema's own independent source of truth for where forward progress sits) — both *after* restoring the snapshot's other fields, so the era can no longer be redirected by anything the snapshot's own copy of it says. Pinned by three tests exercising exactly this: a bogus `era_snapshots` key, a tampered snapshot `city.era` on entry, and a tampered `parked_state.city.era` on exit.
- **`log.Chronicle.restore()` crashed on an unhashable `researched_seen` entry.** `researched_seen` is always a set of plain node-id strings in real play, but `restore()` handed the loaded list straight to `set(...)` with no type-check first — a hand-edited save putting a dict or a list in that list (instead of a string) raised `TypeError: unhashable type` before the entry was ever inspected, taking down the *entire* `load_state()` call (including the perfectly good city/research/era data alongside it) over one bad entry buried in the log. Fixed by filtering to `isinstance(nid, str)` before the set is built, the same "only trust the shape this field can legitimately have" discipline `research.py`'s own `ResearchTree.restore()` already applies to its `researched` list.

**Confirmed already clean, no fix needed** — investigated directly, not assumed:

- **The "save from a later build, with a role/building/resource/research-node key this build doesn't recognise yet" direction.** Already correct by construction: `CITY_KEYED_DICTS`' restore only ever iterates the *live* dict's own keys (`for key in live: ...`), never the save's, so an extra key from a hypothetical future build (`"space_marines"` in `allocation`, `"antimatter"` in `resources`, etc.) is silently dropped rather than crashing anything; `ResearchTree.restore()` already filters to `self.nodes`, so an unrecognised research-node id is dropped the same way. There was no test pinning this direction specifically (only the reverse — an *old* save missing keys the current build has), so `test_load_state_drops_keys_from_a_hypothetical_later_build` was added to close that gap, but no code changed.
- **Snapshot staleness across all six real transitions.** The specific concern CLAUDE.md's Phase 4 description raised by name: does a snapshot taken at Tribal's own transition still read correctly after Classical/Medieval/Industrial/Digital/Space Age have all since added their own global tables (`ERA_ROLES`, `ERA_BUILDINGS`, `NEUTRAL_EFFECTS` keys)? Confirmed clean by actually playing a settlement through all six real transitions via the shipped UI (`test_an_early_snapshot_restores_correctly_after_all_six_real_transitions`, condensing `test_space_age_era.py`'s own `push_to_space()` sequence) and then revisiting the Tribal snapshot from Space Age: because `ROLES`/`BUILDINGS`/`allocation`/`buildings` have been cumulative dicts holding every era's keys (all future ones sitting at 0) since Milestone 8, the Tribal snapshot's own `allocation`/`buildings` dicts already carried `architects: 0`/`habitat_rings: 0` at the time they were taken — restoring it correctly zeroes out Space Age's own building/role investment rather than leaking it backward, and restoring back out of the revisit correctly restores the Space Age numbers untouched. No `KeyError`, no wrong-default behavior, and the sustainability score computed mid-revisit is well-formed (not NaN, within 0..100) even with every later era's global constants in scope. This held by construction, not by luck — it's the direct payoff of the "roles/buildings accumulate, never swap" design decision Milestone 8 made specifically because Milestone 4's original audit anticipated this exact scenario.
- **XSS / injected markup in save data.** Every place a save-derived string reaches the DOM (log entry text, season-report narration, etc.) is written through `.innerText`, never `.innerHTML` — confirmed by grep across `game.py` — so a malicious string in a save's log entries or report text renders as inert literal text, never executable markup. This game has no auth/PII at stake regardless, but this was still worth checking directly rather than assuming.
- **Every era's own "old save missing this era's keys" round-trip** — Classical, Medieval, Industrial, Digital and Space Age each already had a dedicated test of exactly this shape from their own milestone (`test_an_old_save_missing_<era>_keys_loads_cleanly_with_defaults`). Agrarian was the one gap — Milestone 8 (which invented `CITY_KEYED_DICTS`' key-by-key restore in the first place) never wrote its own dedicated version of this test, only a round-trip-preserves-state one. Not a bug (the mechanism is shared and was already proven generically), but a coverage gap worth closing for parity with every era that came after it — `test_an_old_save_missing_agrarian_keys_loads_cleanly_with_defaults` added to `tests/test_agrarian_era.py`.

**A deliberate non-fix, documented rather than left silent:** `CityState.score_history` is restored with NaN/±inf entries filtered out (via the same sanitizer), but its length is left uncapped, unlike `log.Chronicle`'s own `MAX_ENTRIES` cap. This isn't a live issue today — nothing currently reads or renders `score_history` (per `sim.py`'s own comment, it's banked for a future graph) — so there's no current failure mode an unbounded list actually causes, and adding a cap now would be guessing at a number for a feature that doesn't exist yet. Worth revisiting if/when something starts rendering it.

## Tech notes

- Python/Pyodide for all game state and logic, per hub conventions — testable, consistent. This game is significantly larger in scope than any single climate-quartet game, which is why the simulation core, research tree, and save system are already split into clearly separated modules (see Phase 1 build decisions) rather than one `game.py`.
- Three.js for the low-poly 3D rendering layer (Phase 5) — state and rendering kept cleanly separated so the Python side remains fully testable independent of the visual layer.
- Testing is mandatory per hub conventions — prioritize the shared engines (simulation core, research tree, save system) most heavily, since bugs there compound across all seven eras. Maximize coverage, not just adequate coverage — see Phase 6.
- **Space-theme visual pass (matching hub/SOL) — a stopgap, not the final visual direction (see Core system 7 above).** `index.html` now includes `shared/space-bg.css` and the standard `<div class="space-bg">` markup right after `<body>`; `style.css` turned `body`/`#game` transparent so the starfield/nebula shows through, and every `.section` (status, sustainability, work, buildings, research) became its own glass panel — translucent gradient background, `backdrop-filter: blur(14px)`, violet-tinted `rgba(140,160,255,0.14)` border, soft drop shadow — rather than one giant card, matching this game's already-panelled layout. `.row` dividers and the h1 title's gradient-glow treatment followed the same pattern as SOL/the hub, but the warm amber/bronze accent (buttons, era-line, primary CTA) was kept rather than switched to SOL's blue, since that hue is Continuum's own identity. `button.secondary`/`button.primary` moved from flat fills to two-stop gradients of the same hue with a glossy top highlight and a `brightness(1.1)` hover; every existing `:disabled`/`.research-row--done`/`.research-row--locked` state and the locked-node reason text were re-skinned in place, not restructured — no CSS class/id was renamed, and no state-encoding color (land health, the four sustainability components, the weakest-component call-out, the warning/locked-reason amber) was touched, only chrome around them. `.meter-fill--land`/`.meter-fill--score` kept their exact hues and gained a matching-color glow. Verified against the pytest suite (99 passed, unchanged before/after) and live under Pyodide: starfield/nebula render, all four hub panels read as glass, season data and the research tree (including locked-reason text) render correctly, only console output is the pre-existing service-worker-registration error common to every hub game in the preview sandbox.

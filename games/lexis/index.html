Lexis

You are the communications officer. Planet 1 is sending something. Work out what it means, then answer.

Crew log

What the crew says as each world answers. Newest first. The story never tells you what a sign means.

Contact report

The one place Lexis says what each sign really meant and which of your notebook entries were right. It stays here for you to read any time.

About Lexis

    Achievements

      Planet 1: the quiet beacon

      Received transmissions

      Each signal is a row of marks: a short tick or a long bar. Watch what the station does when one arrives.

        Notebook

        Write what you think each group of marks means. Guesses are free and nothing here is marked right or wrong. Tick some entries and press Check to learn how many of them are right, but not which.

          Transmit

          Build a signal from marks, or drop in a group you have already seen. The station answers in its own terms.

          Planet 2: the market world

          A tray sits on the counter. Signs here are made of parts. Watch what arrives on the tray when a sign is sent.

          Received transmissions

            Notebook

            Each part of a sign has its own meaning. Write what you think each part means. Nothing is marked; Check says how many ticked entries are right, not which.

              Transmit

              Build a sign from parts you have seen, then send it.

              Planet 3: the counting station

              A counter shows how many of each thing the station holds. Signs here are nouns you know, numbers you know, and small new signs that change what a message means.

              Received transmissions

              A transmission is a thing, sometimes a small sign after it, and sometimes a number. Watch the counter and listen to what the station says.

                Notebook

                Write what you think each small sign does to a message. Nothing is marked; Check says how many ticked entries are right, not which.

                  Transmit

                  Build a message in order: a thing, then a small sign if you want one, then a number if the message needs one. Then send it.

Charter perks

i

Every time you renew the charter you earn charter points. Spend them here on small, permanent perks that carry into every later charter. Each perk is bought once, deeper perks in a branch need the one before it, and the cost rises with each tier. They are modest tweaks, not stacking multipliers.

    Fleet Captains

    i

    A ship that becomes a veteran hauler (five round trips on one route) earns a captain's seat for the rest of the charter. You choose which of the four captains to post there. Every captain has a perk and a trade-off, serves on one ship at a time, and can be moved to a different ship once per charter. Nothing is random.

    Trade Guild

    i

    Now and then the guild offers a bulk contract: deliver a number of units of a good to a colony that needs it before a deadline. You still sell the goods as normal — the contract is an extra bonus. Declining, ignoring or missing one costs nothing.

    Research

    i

    Research points build up on their own over time, separately from your trading profit — they're a second currency spent only on these permanent, fleet-wide upgrades, not on ships or routes.

    Market prices

    i

    Selling a good repeatedly crashes its price — each unit sold nudges that good's price down, and it only recovers gradually on its own if you leave it alone for a while. Spreading sales across goods keeps prices higher than dumping everything on one.

    Colonies

    i

    A colony's output scales with how well its need is being met, from 0.5x up to 1.5x — an under-supplied colony produces less to trade. Keep delivering enough of what it needs and it develops to Level 2, gaining a second need that reaches into the other supply cycle.

    Fleet priority

    i

    Only affects automated ships that are idle and empty. When on, they'll abandon their fixed shuttle route and reposition themselves toward whichever producer feeds the fleet's most under-served colony, instead of always reloading the same local good.

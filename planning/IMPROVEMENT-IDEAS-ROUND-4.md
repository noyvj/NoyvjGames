# Improvement ideas, Round 4

Written 2026-10-09. Two kinds of section for every game, kept apart on purpose (like Round 3's "gamified" sections): **general ideas** (would make the game better for any player; not shaped by your taste) and **made for you** (built from `planning/PLAYER-PROFILE.md`: your collecting, helper-role, three-goals, dark space-and-robots, calm-watching and short-win tastes). The first two sections are for the whole site. Each idea is one yes / later / no item with an optional comment; "yes" goes into `planning/TODO.md`, "later" into `planning/LATER.md`. Ideas that read facts from a named outside source (for example NASA pages) need that source confirmed before anything is built.

Item counts are scaled to how finished each game is. Every idea was checked against the existing TODO, LATER and Rounds 2 and 3 so none repeats, but the audit was by search, so tell me if one already exists.

## XG. Whole site: general ideas

1. **[SMALL]** Term popovers: one shared helper so any game can mark a word, and tapping it shows a one-line plain definition. You get help with jargon in every game without leaving the screen.
2. **[SMALL]** Replay the tips: one shared button in each game's settings that brings back the one-time hints you have already dismissed. You get a way back to guidance after a break.
3. **[BIG]** Compare two runs: one shared screen that puts any two finished runs side by side with the differences highlighted. You get the same useful tool in every game that keeps run records.
4. **[BIG]** Run report card: one shared end-of-run screen with your best moment, your worst moment and three choices you might redo. You get the same helpful ending everywhere.
5. **[BIG]** Text view for drawings: a shared helper that describes any drawn scene or map as plain text and tables. You get a way for screen-reader users to follow games that rely on pictures.
6. **[SMALL]** Colour-blind palette presets: a shared setting with palettes tuned for the three common kinds of colour blindness. You get readable colour in every game with one choice.
7. **[SMALL]** New feature dots: a small dot on any button added since your last visit, which fades once you open it. You get a quiet way to spot what changed.
8. **[SMALL]** Try next: a card at the end of a run that suggests one other game on the site that fits what you just enjoyed. You get a natural next step.
9. **[BIG]** Difficulty tier picker: one shared picker with named tiers, a plain summary of what each changes and your best result per tier. You get the same clear choice in every game that has hard modes.
10. **[BIG]** Gamepad support: a shared helper so menus and boards can be moved with a controller. You get a comfortable way to play on a TV or from the sofa.
11. **[BIG]** Plain-words option: a setting that swaps long or technical text for shorter, simpler wording. You get games that are easier to read for everyone.
12. **[SMALL]** Extra themes: more colour themes beyond light and dark, such as a warm sepia and a high-contrast amber. You get a choice that suits your eyes and your room.
13. **[SMALL]** Loading tips: while a game loads, show a short tip and the last thing you did there. You get something useful to read during the wait.
14. **[SMALL]** Run notes: a one-line note you can attach to any save or finished run, such as what you were trying. You get a way to remember your own ideas.
15. **[SMALL]** Photo mode: one shared button that hides all menus for a clean screenshot. You get nice pictures of your work from any game.
16. **[SMALL]** Help search: pressing the help key opens a search box over every help panel in the current game. You get an answer in seconds without scrolling.
17. **[SMALL]** Consistent menu corner: the menu, settings and help buttons sit in the same place in every game. You get less hunting when you switch between games.

## SO. SOL: general ideas

1. **[SMALL]** Add a "why is this slow?" button on any resource or building that names the one thing holding it back (for example "Sky Cities on Venus are waiting on 28 more Mars Regolith") and jumps you to the fix. You stop guessing and always know the next useful step.
2. **[BIG]** Add an orbital depot: a shared warehouse where any world can park surplus and any other world can draw from it, upgradeable in size. You get a simple way to smooth out shortages without setting up a route for every pair of worlds.
3. **[BIG]** Give every world one signature building with its own special rule (for example a Venus scrubber that makes neighbouring worlds' ecology decay slower). Each world then plays a little differently and you have a reason to revisit finished worlds.
4. **[BIG]** Add fork nodes to the research tree: at a few points you pick one of two upgrades, and you can swap later for a small fee so no choice is permanent. You get meaningful build choices without any risk of getting stuck.
5. **[SMALL]** Add an "auto-pause on important moments" setting that pauses the game when a research finishes, a world completes, or ecology drops under 15%. You can leave the game running and never miss the moment that matters.
6. **[SMALL]** Add a trade map in the Overview showing which worlds ship what to which, with thicker lines for bigger flows. You see the whole economy at a glance instead of opening each route.
7. **[SMALL]** Add a soft early warning when ecology is heading toward the penalty zone at the current rate ("Ecology reaches 10% in about 40 ticks") with the cheapest fix suggested. You can prevent trouble instead of reacting to it.
8. **[SMALL]** Show "affordable in about N seconds" next to every Buy button, based on current income. You can plan purchases instead of watching numbers.
9. **[SMALL]** Add a hover preview on each world in the Overview showing what finishing its terraforming will unlock. You can choose which world to focus on for a reason.
10. **[SMALL]** Let finished worlds collapse to a single quiet line in the Overview, with a tap to expand. The screen stays tidy in the late game and you still reach any world in one tap.

## CA. Canopy: general ideas

1. **[BIG]** Add a river across the forest: plots next to the water grow faster, and clearing upstream plots dries out the ones downstream. You see how one decision travels across the map.
2. **[BIG]** Add access roads: clearing plots that touch a road is cheaper, but each road splits the forest and lowers wildlife counts on both sides. You get a real spatial trade-off to plan around.
3. **[BIG]** Add natural regrowth: a bare plot beside three or more mature plots slowly recovers for free, with visible drifting seeds. You see that protecting an intact core helps the whole forest heal.
4. **[BIG]** Add two new regions, a boreal forest and a tropical rainforest, each with its own growth speed, wildlife list and one main real-world cause of loss with a named source. You get fresh maps to replay with different rhythms.
5. **[SMALL]** Add a meadow strip option for a cleared plot: a cheap, quick habitat that earns a little biodiversity but little timber value. You get a middle path between clearing and full replanting.
6. **[SMALL]** Add a paint tool: pick Clear, Replant or Adopt, then drag across many plots to apply it. Big forests become quick to manage.
7. **[SMALL]** Show a projected outcome on each stakeholder request ("accepting changes your value by about -40 over 10 seasons") before you choose. You can make informed choices instead of guessing.
8. **[SMALL]** Add a carbon readout that converts your standing forest into tonnes of CO2 stored, using a per-hectare figure read live from a named source. You see your forest's size in real-world terms.
9. **[SMALL]** Extend undo to replant and preserve actions as well as clearing, for a short time. Misclicks stop being costly.
10. **[SMALL]** Add an age overlay that tints each plot by how old its trees are, with the age printed on it. You can spot which parts of the forest are fragile young growth.
11. **[SMALL]** Show tree rings when you tap a mature plot: one ring per season it has survived, with a notch for each nearby clearing. You get a quiet, readable history of each plot.

## LO. Loop: general ideas

1. **[SMALL]** Chain autopsy: after a chain closes, one panel points at the single cycle where you left the most savings on the table and shows the cheapest buy that would have fixed it. You get a clear "do this next time" lesson for your next chain.
2. **[SMALL]** Closing replay: when a chain closes, a short slow-motion replay redraws the whole ring filling up cycle by cycle. You get a satisfying victory scene to watch or skip.
3. **[SMALL]** Supplier choice: every few cycles you pick between two suppliers, one cheaper but dirtier and one dearer but cleaner, with both effects shown before you commit. You get a small real trade-off to weigh without any luck involved.
4. **[BIG]** Second-hand market: instead of keeping a recovered unit in the loop you can sell it on to a second-hand market for funds at a lower price than new. You get a second way to earn money, and a tension between cash now and a tighter loop.

6. **[BIG]** A fourth lever called Reduce: spend to make the product lighter so the same output needs fewer units, which cuts extraction without raising the circular share. You get a second route to winning to compare against closing the loop.
7. **[BIG]** Busy and quiet seasons: demand rises and falls on a visible repeating rhythm, and a warehouse lets you store spare recovered material to cover the busy cycles. You get something to plan around that is fully predictable.
8. **[SMALL]** Focus view: tap any ring node and everything unrelated dims so you can follow just that node's flows in and out. You get a much easier read of a busy chain.
9. **[SMALL]** Partner favours: trade partners you deal with steadily grow friendlier and offer slightly better rates, shown as a small trust bar per partner. You get a reason to stick with a partner instead of hopping between them.
10. **[BIG]** Sorting bench: an optional little puzzle where you sort a pile of broken items into repair, reuse and recycle bins, and each good sort adds a small boost to that lever next cycle. You get a hands-on break from menu clicking that visibly helps the chain.
11. **[BIG]** Workshop floor view: a small top-down scene of your workshop that gains a visible machine for each lever you buy, with items moving between them. You get a picture of your chain that feels like a place.
12. **[SMALL]** Certification ladder: keep a clean streak of low extraction for a set number of cycles to earn Bronze, Silver and Gold chain certificates that give a small permanent price bonus on that chain. You get steady mini-goals between the big milestones.
13. **[SMALL]** Pilot programme: pay a small fee to trial a lever for three cycles at reduced strength before committing to the full price. You get a safe way to test a strategy and still learn from it.
14. **[SMALL]** Half-time score line: during a chain a small line says "if the chain ended now your score would be this, and this is what moves it most". You get a clear sense of how you are doing before the end screen.

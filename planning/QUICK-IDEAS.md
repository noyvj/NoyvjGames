# Quick ideas: many small yes / later / no items

Written 2026-10-09 from `planning/PLAYER-PROFILE.md`, because you said you are enjoying answering. Every item is one idea, one tap (yes / later / no) plus an optional comment. They are all checked against your taste: no roguelike or card mechanics, no timers, energy or lost progress, nothing that punishes missing a day, a fail always lets you restore, hard modes only as options, dark and moody low-poly where there is art, space, robots, computers and science fiction first, helper and collector roles, friends optional. "Yes" means I add it to the TODO, "later" parks it, "no" drops it.

## A. New small puzzle games (each would be its own small game on the site)

1. **[BIG]** Logic Gates: wire AND, OR, NOT and XOR chips to hit target outputs. Each solved level unlocks a new chip, and the last levels have you build a tiny working computer.
2. **[BIG]** Hull Repair: Flow-Free-style routing of power and pipes across a damaged space-station hull. Every solved board repairs one room on a station map, so the puzzles form a bigger picture.
3. **[BIG]** Robot Script: give a robot a short list of instructions to clear a room (Lightbot style). No timer, medals for using fewer steps, and a free sandbox once you finish.
4. **[SMALL]** Reaction Bench: balance chemical reactions to synthesise compounds, and fill a periodic table as you discover each element's uses.
5. **[SMALL]** Rule Finder: a sequence of numbers or shapes follows a hidden rule; you test guesses and name the rule.
6. **[BIG]** Slingshot: plan fuel burns and gravity assists to reach moons in a simple but real solar-system model, with the planet data read from a named source.
7. **[SMALL]** Teach the Machine: train a tiny classifier by choosing which features it looks at, then see exactly why it gets some cases wrong.
8. **[SMALL]** Constellations: connect stars by rules to complete real sky maps, collecting each constellation with a short sourced note.
9. **[SMALL]** Zero-G Shift: Sokoban-style crate pushing, but everything keeps sliding until it hits something.
10. **[SMALL]** Mirror Lab: place mirrors and prisms so a laser reaches every sensor.
11. **[SMALL]** Radio Decode: decode binary, hex and simple ciphers from a drifting signal, each message a short log entry.
12. **[SMALL]** Picture Grid: nonograms where each solved picture goes into a gallery you can browse.

## B. Helper and caretaker games (you lean to the helper role)

1. **[BIG]** Station Medic: help the crew of a space station by solving triage puzzles with limited supplies. Nobody dies on screen; a bad call means "restore and try a different plan".
2. **[BIG]** Robot Repair Shop: fix quirky robots by solving their fault puzzles; each repaired robot becomes a friend that gives you a gift later.
3. **[SMALL]** Fog Rescue: guide lost animals or travellers out of a dark foggy forest by lighting the right lanterns in the right order.
4. **[BIG]** Water Works: route clean water to villages with trade-offs between town, farm and wild land, shown from several people's points of view.
5. **[SMALL]** Derelict Garden: tend a garden on an abandoned ship, restoring one section at a time, with a steady-progress-that-cannot-be-lost feel.
6. **[SMALL]** Memory Archivist: restore corrupted memories from a damaged AI by putting scraps back in order (dark, quiet, science fiction).
7. **[SMALL]** Food Bank: plan a week of meals and deliveries for a food bank with a limited budget and several families' needs.
8. **[SMALL]** Housing Board: allocate a limited set of homes between competing needs and see how each side judges the result.

## C. Collector and completionist ideas

1. **[BIG]** Anomaly Catalogue: a fixed set of odd rooms or screens where you spot what changed and file it in a field catalogue (the kind of anomaly games you like to watch).
2. **[BIG]** Loadout Lab: combine parts to reach target numbers, with a catalogue of every combination you have found, including the one overpowered build per challenge.
3. **[SMALL]** Glitch Hunter: small levels with deliberate, harmless exploits; the goal is to break the rules and the log records every glitch you found.
4. **[SMALL]** Space Museum: curate a museum of objects you have collected across the site, arranged in rooms.
5. **[BIG]** Site Codex: one notebook that fills as you play every game (concepts, facts, characters), with a completion percentage.
6. **[BIG]** Trophy Room: a low-poly room on the hub showing a model for each thing you have earned or finished across the site.
7. **[SMALL]** A "100% map" page: every game as a node that fills as you complete it, with a clear list of what is left.
8. **[SMALL]** A "rarest things you own" strip on the hub, using the existing achievement rarity numbers.

## D. Story and branching-choice games (Lifeline style)

1. **[BIG]** Stranded: you advise a stranded astronaut by text over many branching days; there is no waiting timer, and you can go back and try a different path.
2. **[BIG]** The Quiet Program: advise a broken but gentle program on a failing computer network (a tragic, quiet character in the spirit of the one you like).
3. **[SMALL]** Night Cameras: investigate odd events by switching between security cameras; no jump scares, and the answer is a deduction.
4. **[BIG]** Three Sides of the Story: a branching story about a real issue (for example a water shortage), played once from each of three people's viewpoints.
5. **[BIG]** Evidence Hunt: a Phasmophobia-lite deduction game where you collect evidence and name the ghost, solo first with an optional co-op by invite code.
6. **[SMALL]** Terminal Logs: a choose-your-own-adventure told only through a computer terminal's logs and your commands.

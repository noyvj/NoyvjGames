# Player profile: the owner's taste, from the "About you" survey (answered 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09, all 93 answers)

Source: `planning/PLAYER-SURVEY.md` (the questions), answers read from the owner notes. Check every new idea round, game plan and agent brief against this file before building. It is a summary of what the owner said, not an interpretation beyond it; open questions for them are the "Pf" items in `planning/FOR-YOU.md`.

## What they love (play, watch, read)
- **Warframe (about 3,000 hours)** is the reference game: many ways to play inside one game (collector, character designer, loot and shoot, story, optimiser), fun alone and with others, easy to play half-asleep. Also Destiny 2, Portal 2 (favourite soundtrack, replayed for story nuance), Phasmophobia, Among Us (best gaming memory, banter with friends), Lifeline (choose-your-own-adventure with branching paths), Flow Free, Duolingo, Universal Paperclips, the Duck Life games.
- **Genres:** puzzle and looter shooters first; sims and tycoons, horror and watching city builders, idle games and strategy as well. Spatial and logic puzzles most, but all puzzle kinds.
- **Best-feeling actions:** collecting and solving. They collect everything; lore matters least unless it is a notebook being filled in.
- **100% feels good** when achievements are reachable and represent how much of the game has been explored and understood.
- **Wants:** objectives without a strict order (three "get X to level 5 to unlock Z" goals visible at all times is the ideal), friendly welcome-back, intuitive games that need no tutorial, hard modes that are options and never the base game, easy-to-medium base difficulty.
- **Watches:** long commentary let's plays (2 hours or more) of 100% runs, glitch and build-breaking, horror and anomaly games; Ryan George and film or show reactions. Humour: dark; absurd goes too far and "brainrot" references annoy them.
- **Reads and watches:** Marvel and science fiction (likes fiction that explains science that does not exist yet), dystopias, fanfiction (skips long world-building). Fan of Tron's Rinzler: tragic, quiet, broken-savior characters.

## What they dislike (do not build)
- **Roguelikes and deck or card games**: randomness that limits planning three steps ahead, and hard-lose states. Luck is fine only as small multipliers, random events like Aftermath's, and random spawn locations.
- **Timers, energy limits, grinding, losing progress** (hard fail is acceptable only in an opt-in "ironman" mode). A fail should mean "you failed, kind of, here is how to restore progress", not a restart.
- Once-a-day mechanics that make something important unobtainable if missed (daily content is nice, daily pressure is not).
- Visual novels and card-based games; bright chaotic fantasy (gothic is fine); "obvious hero" characters who are all good and never make a mistake.
- Being the worst on a board: leaderboards must stay optional (they do not care about being best, but their friends do).
- Perfectionism turning a game into a job (they burn out).

## How they play
- PC mostly, 10pm to 6am, often from bed; phone in class or half asleep. Sessions are **under 20 minutes or over 3 hours, nothing in between**.
- Improviser who looks up a walkthrough or meta build when stuck (quits if under an hour invested). Predictability over surprises. Feels clever finding the one broken build; proud completing a self-set session goal; relaxed when confident in a thing (they asked for examples: see Pf1); stressed when their ability brings someone else down. Prefers helper or healer roles; wants to create or help rather than compete.
- Short wins that build into a slow arc (many short missions that form a storyline; puzzles that form a bigger picture).
- Plays many games mainly because friends do (Among Us, CSGO, Overwatch, Dead by Daylight) and wants some tie like that on the site, optional. Does not like sharing screenshots or share codes themselves.
- Play-tests the site to build a list of things to improve; favourite memory of own games: grinding Canopy achievements. Most played on the site now: Le Champ de Mots (French exam soon), SOL, Continuum (class).

## Themes, look, tone
- **Themes:** space, computers, robots, science fiction; the ocean only when creepy or dark. Wants teaching games to continue but "more game than teaching" after the teaching core exists (already heavy on climate, languages and history). New subject interests: coding, maths, AI, chemistry, physics.
- **Look:** dark and moody (earthy sometimes), low-poly is a safe default, hand-drawn on occasion, pixel art only if the game makes sense with it; they will accept rough graphics if needed.
- **Tone:** a mix. Story only if it is Lifeline-style choose-your-own-adventure with a lot of branching, and optional unless the story is the whole game. Real-world issue games should present multiple points of view to compare, not a single stance. Cares about food, healthcare, water, housing, body autonomy, LGBTQ, women's and racial rights.
- **Audience:** them and their friends for now, strangers later. First minute should feel like "no matter who you are there is a game for you". Accessible to everyone (the owner has no needs themselves).

## Three words for the site (H6)
Diverse, rewarding, engaging.

## Proudest and least sure (H10)
They have not played the games enough yet to say.

## Their dream game (H5, shortened)
A city builder in a dark foggy forest that has everything: first-person nodes to help others, gather resources, hunt or defend; story quests; idle and optimisation in the city; a mining mode with a huge skill tree and bunker sections that become building specialisations; each building has its own puzzle whose levels give permanent upgrades; friendship sidequests and gifting with the people who run each building. All the play styles matter but none is overbearing.

## How they want me to work (I section)
- After an ideas list is done, "you decide" means use judgement. Small spec tweaks (10% to 15% because it makes more sense) are mine to just do unless they stated the number themselves, not as "something like 20%".
- When unsure whether something fits their taste: **ask, and give safe alternative options** alongside.
- Ideas sheets: **many quick items is good**; scale the number of questions to how finished a game is; do not repeat the same question across many games: if something applies to **more than 4 games, put it in the cross-game section**.
- No complaints about how work and questions have been going.

## Added 2026-10-09 (answers to my follow-ups)
- **What feels relaxed:** steady progress that can never be lost, and a routine they know by heart. Calm modes lean on those.
- Said yes to: a Lifeline-style story game, coding or logic puzzle games, a shared three-goals panel for idle games, a fast optional reaction game, optional friend ties, the no-all-good-hero rule.
- The foggy-forest dream game is their huge endgame: they want a lot of questions and their own decisions before any planning doc, and are happy for parts (the per-building puzzles) to be built as separate games first.

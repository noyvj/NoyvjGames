Sources & Credits

The real-world research behind every game that draws on one — gathered in one place instead of scattered across nine "The Real Story" panels.

Nine of the twelve NoyvjGames titles are built around a real climate or civic issue, and each one cites the actual research its mechanics are grounded in — the eight climate-quartet games via an in-game "The Real Story" panel, and Continuum via a per-era real-world info panel. This page is that same content, pulled together across every game so it can be read (or checked) in one place. SOL, Trade Empire and Le Champ de Mots aren't listed below — they're fictional sandboxes / a language-study tool, not built around a specific real-world source base.

This is a plain hand-written page, not a live-parsed one like What's New or Roadmap — those two pull from a dev log and a CLAUDE.md table, both plain text a browser can fetch and parse directly. A game's actual source list lives inside its Python game.py (or, for Continuum, info_content.py), and reliably parsing a Python dict literal out of source text in client-side JavaScript would be far more fragile than the payoff is worth for a list that changes only when a game's research base changes — rare, and always a deliberate edit. So this page is copied from that same canonical Python data by hand, kept in sync manually going forward, same as this hub's other few fully static pages (Terms & Privacy). Every link below was live in each game's own source list at the time it was added there.

Canopy

Deforestation & carbon sinks

  • World Resources Institute — Forests in the IPCC Special Report on Land Use: 7 Things to Know

    Explains why deforestation and forest carbon sinks are two sides of the same coin — maps directly to Canopy's clear/preserve tension.

  • World Resources Institute — How Effective Is Land At Removing Carbon Pollution? The IPCC Weighs In

    Real reforestation carbon-removal potential — grounds the "replanting works, just slower" hope angle in actual IPCC figures.

  • UNFCCC — Land Use, Land-Use Change and Forestry (LULUCF)

    The formal policy framework for tracking forest carbon sinks internationally — why Canopy treats plots as carbon-relevant assets, not scenery.

  • Climate Change Resources — Deforestation & Reforestation

    Accessible overview with links to real reforestation organizations, for players who want to go from facts to action.

Grid

Energy transition & the renewable cost curve

  • IEA — Rapid rollout of clean technologies makes energy cheaper, not more costly

    Real-world backing for Grid's central claim: leaning renewable is the cheaper long-run path, not a sacrifice.

  • Oxford Institute for Energy Studies — A critical assessment of learning curves for solar and wind

    A balanced, critical look at the same cost-decline concept Grid's core mechanic is built on.

  • US DOE / Lawrence Berkeley National Lab — Learning a Better Way To Forecast Wind and Solar Energy Costs

    The actual learning-rate figures (wind ~15%, solar ~24% per capacity doubling) behind Grid's cost curve.

  • IEA — Breakthrough Agenda Report 2025: Power

    Current real-world electricity cost figures, grounding the global-comparison line.

Tide

Ocean acidification & sea-level rise

  • NOAA Fisheries — Understanding Ocean Acidification

    Explains the shellfish/reef impact mechanism directly — the real process behind Tide's fish-stock crash.

  • NASA Sea Level Change Portal — Global Mean Sea Level

    Real satellite-measured sea-level data and rate-of-rise figures, informing the pacing of Tide's background timeline.

  • NOAA Climate.gov — Climate Change: Global Sea Level

    Explains both causes of sea-level rise (thermal expansion + ice melt) in plain language.

  • Smithsonian Ocean Portal — Ocean Acidification

    A clear, visual explanation of the acidification chemistry, most accessible of the four sources.

Aftermath

Climate adaptation & resilience

  • IPCC AR6 Working Group II — Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability

    The authoritative global reference on adaptation as a distinct climate response, backing Aftermath's core framing.

  • World Resources Institute — Accelerating Climate-resilient Infrastructure Investment in China

    A real resilience-infrastructure investment case study, grounding Aftermath's resource-allocation mechanic.

  • World Resources Institute — Driving System Shifts for Climate Resilience (Bhutan, Ethiopia, Costa Rica)

    Real communities' documented adaptation journeys — strong backing for the "look how far you've come" hope angle.

  • EU Mission on Adaptation to Climate Change — Success Stories

    A running collection of real municipal adaptation wins — concrete "this actually worked" examples.

Herd

Industrial agriculture & methane

  • FAO — Livestock and enteric methane

    The definitive real-world figures behind Herd's core mechanic — agriculture's share of methane emissions and where it comes from.

  • Clean Air Task Force — Accelerating climate solutions in agriculture

    Documents a real ~42% methane-intensity reduction from better farm practices — directly supports Herd's decoupling hope angle.

  • US EPA — Agriculture and Aquaculture: Food for Thought

    Explains why methane's short-lived-but-potent warming profile makes it a distinct lever from CO2.

Thaw

Permafrost & the methane feedback loop

  • MIT Climate Portal — Is methane release from the Arctic unstoppable?

    The clearest source for Thaw's hope angle — frames the feedback loop as a dimmer switch, not an on/off switch.

  • Nature Climate Change — Seasonal increase of methane emissions linked to warming in Siberian tundra

    Real observational evidence (not just modeling) of the feedback loop already measurably happening.

  • WWF Arctic — Thawing permafrost

    An accessible explainer connecting permafrost thaw to real Arctic communities' lived experience.

  • PMC/NCBI — 21st-century modeled permafrost carbon emissions accelerated by abrupt thaw beneath lakes

    A more technical source on abrupt (not just gradual) thaw mechanisms, tying to Thaw's tipping-point moment.

Loop

Circular economy & overconsumption

  • Ellen MacArthur Foundation — The Circular Economy: Definition & Model Explained

    The standard-setting definition of circular economy — Loop's core mechanic is a direct translation of this framework.

  • Ellen MacArthur Foundation — Circular Economy Principles

    Breaks circularity into three concrete design principles, structuring Loop's three types of circularity investment.

  • Mongabay — The circular economy: Sustainable solutions to solve planetary overshoot?

    Accessible journalism with a concrete example (recycled steel's emissions/water savings) for the framing paragraph.

  • PMC/NCBI — Waste metrics in the framework of circular economy

    A more academic treatment connecting overconsumption directly to circular economy metrics.

Drift

Climate migration & displacement

  • UNHCR — Climate change and displacement

    The authoritative agency perspective, framing displacement institutionally — consistent with Drift's own framing.

  • Migration Policy Institute — Climate Migration 101: An Explainer

    Real projections (44-216 million internal migrants by 2050) that echo Drift's "preparedness changes the outcome" hope angle.

  • Migration Policy Institute — Who Counts as a Climate Migrant?

    Explains the legal/definitional gap behind why Drift frames this as a systems/capacity problem, not a legal one.

  • Brookings — The climate crisis, migration, and refugees

    Policy-level analysis of the institutional response gap, grounding Drift's receiving-region capacity mechanic.

  • Migration Policy Institute — How Are Refugees Faring? Integration at U.S. and State Levels

    Source for the in-game real-world resettlement-outcome benchmark: as of 2023, 89% of working-age refugees resettled in the U.S. within the previous five years were employed.

Continuum

Sustainable cities across the ages (SDG 11) — sourced per era, seven eras, four sources each

Tribal

  • Pressbooks Nebraska — "Political Systems" (Introduction to Anthropology)

    The real anthropological distinction between bands and tribes, and how village growth and architecture marked the shift.

  • UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Neolithic Site of Çatalhöyük

    One of the earliest known settlements, notable for its egalitarian layout — a direct anchor for the Tribal era's non-hierarchical starting roles.

  • PMC — "The study of early human settlement preference and settlement prediction in Xinjiang, China"

    How early humans actually chose settlement locations based on geography and environment.

  • Research Outreach / Medium — Dunbar's Number

    The real cognitive/social limit on stable group size (~150, in a layered structure of 5/15/50/150) — calibrates this era's population thresholds.

Agrarian

  • National Geographic — "What was the Neolithic Revolution?"

    How early farmers produced storable surplus in the first place — the basis for this era's surplus/storage mechanics.

  • HISTORY — "Neolithic Revolution"

    The shift from nomadic to settled farming life, with Çatalhöyük as the bridge example.

  • EBSCO Research Starters — "Neolithic Revolution"

    Specialized tools, pottery, and early urbanization as direct consequences of food surplus.

  • TxWes Pressbooks — "The Neolithic Revolution" (World History)

    Surplus specifically creating the first social hierarchies and property accumulation — basis for the surplus-hoarding equity penalty.

Classical

  • The Getty — "Mesopotamia: Civilization Begins"

    Uruk's growth into a walled city of roughly 100,000 via intensive irrigation agriculture directed by temple estates.

  • TheCollector — "What Can Uruk and Ur Teach Us About the Birth of Civilization?"

    Canal management required centralized planning and coordinated labor — why Canals need Administrators to deliver their yield bonus.

  • PLOS One — "Morphodynamic Foundations of Sumer"

    Recent research proposing that natural tidal irrigation, not just human planning, jumpstarted Sumerian urbanization.

  • GlobalSecurity.org — "Uruk Period"

    Concrete scale figures — population around 45,000, a trade network stretching from Turkey to Iran.

Medieval

  • Popular Archaeology — "Inside a Medieval City: Public Health, Housing, and Everyday Regulations"

    Real medieval sanitation regulation — waste fines, zoning toxic trades away from drinking water.

  • SAGE Journals — Coomans & Hermenault, "Public Works, Spatial Strategies, and Mobility in Late Medieval Ghent"

    Academic analysis of medieval public-works investment used specifically to mitigate risk, not to grow output.

  • Johns Hopkins University Press — Magnusson, "Urban Infrastructure in Medieval England: Sustainability and Resilience"

    A recent academic study literally framed around sustainability and resilience in medieval public works.

  • Fiveable — "Urban Revival and the Growth of Cities" (European History 1000-1500)

    Guild specialisation and municipal charters granting real self-governance.

Industrial

  • Britannica — "Urban Planning: Industrialization, Infrastructure, Cities"

    19th-century industrial growth outpacing planning, producing slums and disease before sanitation reform caught up.

  • The Economic Journal (Oxford Academic) — "Coal Smoke, City Growth, and the Costs of the Industrial Revolution"

    Rigorous economic research showing industrial pollution measurably reduced long-run city growth.

  • Sociology.Institute — "The Evolution of Industrial Cities"

    Global urban population rising from 3% (1800) to nearly 50% (2000).

  • PMC — "Bibliometric Analysis and Research Trend Forecast of Healthy Urban Planning for 40 Years"

    How modern urban planning as a discipline emerged directly in response to industrial-era public health crises.

Digital

  • UN DESA — "SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities"

    The official UN publication on this goal — the game's own SDG framing is built around it.

  • Our World in Data — "Make Cities Inclusive, Safe, Resilient and Sustainable"

    Breaks SDG 11 into its actual 10 targets and 16 measurable indicators.

  • National Geographic Education — "Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities"

    Urban areas grew 1.28 times faster than their populations between 2000 and 2014 — the basis for the sprawl mechanic.

  • Agenda 2030 in Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC) — "Sustainable Cities and Communities"

    Concrete present-day figures — 70% of people projected to live in cities by 2050, 1.1 billion currently in slum-like conditions.

  • UN — The Sustainable Development Goals Report 2025 (Current Day)

    The tenth annual global stocktaking of SDG progress: real gains made, but the current pace isn't enough to meet the 2030 targets.

Space Age

  • Big Think — "Urban planning in space: 3 off-world designs for future cities"

    Covers three real design concepts — NASA's 1977 rotating habitat study, MIT's Mars City "Redwood Forest" winner, and ring habitats.

  • Planetizen — "NASA's Urban Plan for Space Settlements"

    Frames NASA's 1977 "Space Settlements: A Design Study" as literally a city-planning policy document.

  • Columbia University Press — Scharmen, "Space Settlements"

    An academic architectural history taking NASA's 1975 Summer Study designs seriously as real design problems.

  • Dagstuhl / SpaceCHI 2025 — "Designing for Usability in Modular Space Habitats: A Space Syntax Perspective"

    Current (2025) research on how modular space habitat layouts affect real usability at scale.
